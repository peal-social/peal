You write a post once in Peal, adjust it where a platform needs something different, and Peal sends it to each of your channels at the time you set. Then each platform's numbers come back, so you can see what worked without opening every app.

<Callout type="warning" title="Peal hasn't been released yet">
  Peal is being built in the open. These pages describe what works today, and
  they grow as features land.
</Callout>

Here's what works today:

- Workspaces, with members and roles.
- Accounts on [fourteen platforms](/platforms), from Mastodon and Bluesky to YouTube and Google Business Profile, channels on Discord, Telegram and Slack, and articles on websites.
- Posts with images and videos, scheduled on a calendar and published.
- A notice of how each post went, and how it did on its platforms.
- A writing assistant.

## What Peal does [#what-it-does]

Each task below is one part of the app:

| Task                      | What Peal does                                                                                                                                        |
| ------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Write once                | You draft a post in one editor, then give it its own text on each channel where it needs to differ. Counters show how much room each platform leaves. |
| Schedule across platforms | You pick the accounts and the time. Peal publishes each post when it's due, in the time zone you scheduled it in, daylight saving included.           |
| See results               | Peal reads back each post's likes, reposts, replies and quotes for a month, so you can see what worked in one place.                                  |
| Work as a team            | Workspaces have members and roles, so several people can write, review and schedule together.                                                         |

<Callout
  type="info"
  title="Peal doesn't post again when a platform doesn't confirm"
>
  When a platform doesn't confirm that a post went out, Peal checks again only
  in ways that can't post it twice. If that still settles nothing, it marks the
  post unconfirmed and asks you to check the platform before you post again.
</Callout>

## Run it yourself [#self-hostable]

Every outside service Peal relies on has a default you can host yourself. A self-hosted instance needs three things:

- Postgres.
- An S3-compatible store.
- A way to send email.

Billing, captcha, AI and telemetry are optional. [Self-hosting](/self-hosting) explains how to run it, and [Environment variables](/self-hosting/environment-variables) lists every setting. The people who run the server find accounts and workspaces in the [staff console](/self-hosting/staff).

## Fair source [#fair-source]

The source is public under the Functional Source License (FSL-1.1-ALv2). Here's what it lets you do:

- Use, modify and self-host Peal for any purpose, except offering it to others as a competing commercial product or service.
- Use each release under the Apache License 2.0 too, two years after it ships.

That's why Peal calls itself fair source rather than open source.
