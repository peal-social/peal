## Tools [#tools]

Each tool acts as you, in the workspace the client signed in to, through the same checks as the web app: your role decides what it may do.

A client is refused what you did not allow it, with a message naming the permission it lacks: every change for a client you let read only, and whatever puts posts out for one you did not let publish. To grant more, connect it again and allow it there.

### Find a tool [#find-tools]

`find_tools` takes an `area` (`account`, `posts`, `tags`, `saved`, `media`, `reports`, `people`, `webhooks`, `connect`, `channels`, `review` or `inbox`), a `query`, both or neither. For each tool it answers:

| Field            | What it says                                                                                                 |
| ---------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| `summary`        | What the tool does                                                                                           |
| `changes`        | Whether it changes something                                                                                 |
| `destructive`    | Whether it destroys or overwrites                                                                            |
| `reachesOutside` | Whether it reaches outside Peal                                                                              |
| `scope`          | The scope it takes                                                                                           |
| `scopeGranted`   | Whether the connection holds every scope it takes; `null` for an API key, which has permissions and no scope |

The tools that spend on ads, or quote, pause or stop a boost, take `workspace:ads`, and spending takes `workspace:publish` beside it.

Each tool is written by hand, with a description of what it changes. None is generated from an endpoint of the API, and the API has endpoints no tool calls. A client that would rather not read every description calls `find_tools` first, and then reads only the tools it chose.

### What a channel takes [#capability-tools]

Two more read [what a channel takes](/developers#capabilities), so a client need not know each platform's rules. They change nothing, and may ask the channel's platform, outside Peal (`openWorldHint`):

| Tool                       | What it answers                                                                                                                                                                                   |
| -------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| `get_channel_capabilities` | How a channel's text is counted and limited, the media it takes, its platform's options field by field, its lookups, whether Peal reads its posts' counts, and what the account itself restricts  |
| `run_channel_lookup`       | A [lookup](/developers#lookups) of a channel: account data its options refer to, such as the categories a YouTube video can be filed under or the boards of a Pinterest account, a page at a time |

<Callout type="warning" title="What “Needs publishing” means">
  It means the permission to schedule and publish (`workspace:publish`), and a
  role that schedules posts: owner, admin or editor. What `schedule_post`,
  `publish_post`, `create_post`, `create_posts` and `repost_target` send is
  public on the platforms, and Peal cannot take it back once it is out.
  `unpublish_post` deletes what went out from the platforms, for everyone, and
  cannot be undone either: a client should call it only when you asked for that
  very post to be taken down.
</Callout>

In a workspace that asks for [approvals](/guides/posts/review#policy), what these tools send or schedule waits instead: the post is answered with the status `awaitingApproval`, and goes out only once a person approves it. `approve_post` and `request_changes` need publishing with a role that approves posts, as approving is what sends a post out.

### Checks before a post goes out [#post-checks]

Before a post is scheduled, it is checked against every channel's platform: text length, media types, sizes and counts, required options, and what the account itself refuses. When anything is wrong, nothing is scheduled, and the refusal lists every violation at once, each with its channel, and its field or media ([How refusals read](/developers/agents#refusals)). `validate_post` answers the same list beforehand, and makes nothing.

`create_post` and `create_posts` also refuse a post whose content (the text the channel receives and the same media, in the same order) already goes to one of its channels less than 23 hours from its time, naming the post that has it. A call made twice by mistake makes no second post. `allowDuplicate: true` lets it through, for when you mean to post the same twice. Scheduling a draft with `schedule_post` is not checked ([Duplicate posts](/developers/posting#duplicates)).

`create_draft`, `update_draft`, `create_post` and `create_posts` take `shortenLinks: true` to send a post's links [shortened](/guides/posts/published#short-links) and `false` to send them as written. Left out, a new post takes the workspace's default (`get_short_link_status`'s `byDefault`) when it is made. No tool shortens an address outside a post.

### Signatures, channel sets and default options [#saved]

A workspace saves three things to write its posts with, and `create_draft`, `update_draft`, `create_post`, `create_posts` and `validate_post` take them:

| What            | Parameter                          | What it does                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                             |
| --------------- | ---------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| Channel sets    | `channelSets`                      | Names sets by id or by name, instead of listing channels in `channelIds` or beside them. The post goes to each set's connected channels. A channel of a set that is no longer connected is left out, and the answer says which in `warnings`, never silently: the client should tell you. A post sent to a set none of whose channels is left, with no other channel, is refused.                                                                                        |
| Signatures      | `signatureId`, `defaultSignatures` | `signatureId` puts a signature under the post's text on every channel, and `defaultSignatures` each channel's default one: the workspace's under the post's text, a platform's or a channel's own under the text that channel has instead. A signature becomes text of the post: it is counted against each platform's limit, and it is never added under a text that already ends with it. A post in several parts (`content.followUps`) is signed under its last part. |
| Default options | none                               | On a channel where a new draft or a new post gives no [platform options](/guides/posts#options), it takes the options a set it names keeps for the channel, then the channel's default options. ### Queues [#queue-tools]

`create_post`, `create_posts` and `validate_post` take `when: { "_tag": "queue", "queue": "…" }` beside `now` and `at`. The post takes the next free slot of the queue named by its id or its name, or of the default queue when `queue` is left out, decided when the call is received. With no `channelIds` and no `channelSets`, it goes to the queue's channels. `validate_post` answers that slot in `queueSlot` ([Queues](/guides/posts/queues#queues)).

### Repeating posts [#recycling-tools]

`recycle_post` makes a scheduled or published post [repeat](/guides/posts/queues#recycling) (`every`, `count` or `until`, `variants`, `queueId`). It publishes again, in public, without anyone being asked each time, so a client calls it only when you asked for the post to repeat.

`list_posts` takes `recyclingId`.

### What no tool hands out [#account-data]

No tool exports your data or lists the applications that act for you. An MCP client passes whatever its tools answer to an AI model, and your export holds your sessions' addresses, your invitations and everything you wrote.

The instance refuses both to every OAuth client but the [command line](/developers/cli/workspace#account), which you sign in at your own terminal and which saves them in your own files. Account settings, in the web app, show them too.
