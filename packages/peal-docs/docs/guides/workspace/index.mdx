A workspace's settings belong to its members, and your role decides which ones you can change. [Your account's settings](/guides/workspace/account) belong to you alone, in every workspace.

## Find the settings [#workspace]

In a workspace, click **Settings** at the foot of the sidebar. The sidebar then lists the settings pages in four groups:

- **Personal**: your own account and notifications, the same in every workspace ([Your account](/guides/workspace/account), [Notifications](/guides/workspace/notifications)), and the agent's skills.
- **Publishing**: **Tags**, **Signatures**, **Channel sets**, **Direct messages** and **Assistant**.
- **Automations**: **Queues**, **Repeating posts**, **Follow-ups**, **Comment replies** and **Feeds**.
- **Administration**: the workspace itself, its members, its security and its billing.

You see the pages your role lets you open. These are the workspace's own:

| Page          | Who sees it                     |
| ------------- | ------------------------------- |
| **Workspace** | Every member                    |
| **Members**   | Every member                    |
| **Security**  | Every member                    |
| **Billing**   | Members who may see billing     |
| **Assistant** | Owners and admins               |
| **API keys**  | Members who may handle keys     |
| **Webhooks**  | Members who may manage webhooks |

**API keys** and **Webhooks** aren't under **Settings**. Switch the sidebar from **PealCreative** to **PealAPI** to find them under **Build**, with **Partner** under **Partners**.

Most settings save as soon as you change them, without a **Save** button. The forms that create something, invite someone or change your password keep their button.

Here's what each role can do in the settings:

| Role        | In the settings                                                                                                                                                               |
| ----------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Owner       | Everything: rename, members, owners included, invitations, API keys, webhooks, the assistant's image and text providers, requiring two-step verification, and deleting it     |
| Admin       | Rename, members other than owners, invitations, API keys, webhooks and the assistant's image and text providers. Never owners, requiring two-step verification or deleting it |
| Editor      | Sees the members                                                                                                                                                              |
| Contributor | Sees the members                                                                                                                                                              |
| Viewer      | Sees the members                                                                                                                                                              |

On **Workspace**, owners and admins type the workspace's **Name** and **Address** in place. Peal saves each one when you leave the field or press Enter. They also set **Approvals**, which decides whether posts wait for an approval before they're scheduled. The choice is saved as soon as you pick it. See [Review and approval](/guides/posts/review#policy).

### Manage members [#members]

**Members** lists everyone in the workspace, with their role. If you're an owner or an admin, you can:

- Change a member's role from the list.
- Click **Remove** to take someone out of the workspace. You confirm first, and they lose access at once.

Only owners can make someone an owner or remove an owner. A workspace always keeps at least one owner, so the last owner can't give up that role or be removed.

A member's [API keys](#api-keys) follow them:

- When a member is removed, leaves the workspace or closes their account, the keys they made in it stop working at once.
- When you give a member a role with fewer permissions, the keys they made that hold a permission the new role doesn't have stop working.

In a [partner account](/developers/embedding#keys)'s home workspace, the partner keys a member made stop working too when they go, or when their new role can't manage the partner account. Before you confirm a removal, or a role change that revokes keys, Peal tells you how many of their keys stop working. Each key revoked this way is kept in the [activity log](#activity) as `apiKeys.revoke` (a partner key as `partnerAccount.revokeKey`), by whoever removed the member or changed the role, or by the member who left.

### Invite people [#invitations]

<Steps>
<Step title="Send the invitation">

On **Members**, under **Invite someone**, type an email address and select a role, then click **Send the invitation**. Only owners can invite owners.

</Step>
<Step title="The person joins">

They get a link to join, valid for 7 days. It's in their language if they already have an account, and in yours otherwise. They join once they're signed in with that address.

</Step>
</Steps>

**Pending invitations**, on the same page, lists them with their expiry date. For each one, you can:

- Click **Send again** to email the link again, valid for 7 more days.
- Click **Revoke** to stop the link working.
- Click **Copy link** to copy the same link, in your language, and hand it over another way.

Only the person invited can accept it, signed in with the invited address once it's verified, and only until the invitation expires.

Each invitation, sent or sent again, is an email. A day (UTC) allows 100 of them for each member who invites, 100 for each workspace, 3 from one workspace to the same address, and 20 to one address from all of Peal. Past that, inviting is refused until the next day (`too_many_invitations`, 409, through the API).

The email shows your name and the workspace's. A name is one line, up to 100 characters for a person and 120 for a workspace, and can't hold `://` or `www.`. A name that reads as a web or email address, such as `Booking.com`, is fine in Peal, but emails say "A member" or "a workspace" instead, so that no email carries a link someone typed.

### Require two-step verification [#require-two-step]

Under **Security**, an owner can require [two-step verification](/guides/workspace/account#two-step) of every member. It takes an owner signed in themselves. An admin can't do it, and nor can an API key, an application or staff acting as an owner. You also need it on for your own account first, so that you can't lock yourself out.

<Callout type="warning" title="Members without it lose access at once">
  Before the requirement takes effect, Peal names the members whose account
  doesn't have it on. They lose access straight away, until they turn it on.
</Callout>

Every member sees whether it's required. Those who manage members see who has it on, in **Members**.

A member whose account doesn't have it on is refused the workspace everywhere they reach it:

- The web app shows them what the owners require and a way to their account settings, instead of any page of the workspace.
- The [command line](/developers/cli#login) and [MCP clients](/developers/mcp#sign-in) are refused with `twoStepRequired`.
- The sign-in service's own workspace routes refuse them for that workspace too.

Their sessions and applications stay signed in, so everything works again as soon as they turn it on. Invitations still work: the person joins, then is asked to turn it on. The workspace's [API keys](#api-keys) and a partner's keys aren't affected, because they belong to the workspace, not to a person.

Turning the requirement off takes effect at once too. Both changes are kept in the [activity log](#activity).

### Create an API key [#api-keys]

<Steps>
<Step title="Create the key">

Under **API keys**, give the new key a name, choose how long it works (30, 90 or 365 days, or until revoked) and what it may do. You can only give it permissions your own role has.

</Step>
<Step title="Copy it">

The key appears once, right after you create it. Copy it then, because Peal keeps only a hash of it. [The API overview](/developers#authentication) shows how to send it.

</Step>
</Steps>

Click **Revoke** to stop a key at once. Staff acting as a member can't see or handle keys.

The list shows who made each key. A key never does more than the member who made it can do, so it stops working once they leave the workspace, are removed or close their account, and once their role no longer has one of its permissions ([Manage members](#members)). A key made before Peal recorded its maker shows **Made before keys recorded who made them**, and keeps working until someone revokes it.

The list says whether each key **Can schedule and publish posts**. It can when it holds `post:schedule`, `post:publish` or `post:approve`. Without them, it can write drafts at most. A post created or scheduled with a key says so on its page, with the key's name.

<Callout type="warning" title="What a key publishes is public">
  Give a key those permissions only when whatever uses it has to publish. What
  it sends is public on the platforms ([Posting from an AI
  agent](/developers/agents#api)).
</Callout>

### Add webhooks [#webhooks]

Under **Webhooks**, owners and admins add the addresses Peal calls when something happens in the workspace, 10 at most. Each endpoint has:

- the events it wants and a description;
- a signing secret, which appears once, right after the endpoint is created or its secret rotated;
- a log of its deliveries, kept for 30 days, from which you can send a delivery again;
- a test event you can send it.

Staff acting as a member can't change webhooks. [Webhooks](/developers/webhooks) lists the events and shows how to verify a delivery.

### Connect your AI providers [#providers]

Under **Assistant**, owners and admins connect the workspace's own AI providers, each with your account's key there. The [writing assistant](/guides/posts/assistant#assistant), the [agent](/guides/agent) and the [images the assistant makes](/guides/media#generate) can then use your account instead of the instance's models, outside the instance's allowance, on any plan. Connecting one takes `assistant:configure`, which an [API key](#api-keys) or a [partner key](/developers/embedding#account) can also be given.

In **AI providers**, click **Connect a provider**, pick it in **Provider** and add your key. Peal knows these by name: Anthropic, OpenAI, Google Gemini, Mistral, xAI, Groq, Together AI, DeepSeek, OpenRouter, Cerebras, Fireworks AI, ElevenLabs and fal, which write no text and make voice-overs, transcripts and, for fal, images, and [Shotstack](#shotstack), which composes slide videos. It fills in each one's address, so you only add the key. ElevenLabs, fal and Shotstack are reached at their own addresses alone, so their dialog has no address. For any other endpoint that speaks the OpenAI chat completions API, the OpenAI images API or both, pick **Another provider** and type its address. Each provider connected is a row, with its address when it isn't the provider's own and **Key saved**. Click **Edit** to change it. The dialog's fields:

| Field        | What to enter                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                          |
| ------------ | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Provider** | One of the providers above, or **Another provider**. For Anthropic, Peal adds `/v1/messages` to the address and sends the key as `x-api-key`. For every other, it adds `/chat/completions` for text and `/images/generations` for images, and sends the key as a bearer token.                                                                                         |
| **Key**      | Your account's key at the provider. Peal keeps it encrypted, for this workspace, this provider and this exact address only. A saved key shows as saved and stays until you type another one.                                                                                                                                                                           |
| **Address**  | The provider's own address, which the dialog fills in for you and keeps under **Advanced**. Change it only to go through a proxy of your own. For **Another provider**, the address `/chat/completions` and `/images/generations` follow, such as `https://ai.example.com/v1`. HTTPS on the default port, on a public host, with no user, password, query or fragment. |

Peal never shows the key again and never writes it to its logs. Any change to the address, its host or its path asks for the key again, because Peal never sends a key to an address it wasn't given for. Peal doesn't call the provider when you save.

- Click **Test**, for a provider that writes text, to send it one short request, the rewrite of a fixed sentence, with the model the workspace writes with there, or the first Peal suggests. Peal says whether it answered with a suggestion the assistant can use, or why not. The test counts as one of the workspace's own requests that day.
- When the instance's keys change and the saved key can't be read any more, the row says **Key unreadable** and the dialog asks for it again.
- Click **Remove** and confirm to delete the key. What the provider was used for goes back to the instance's models, if it has them, within the workspace's allowance.

The [command line](/developers/cli/posts#assistant) has `peal assistant providers`. No MCP tool reads or sets them, because a key typed into a chat reaches a model.

<AccordionItem title="Addresses Peal refuses">

Peal refuses an address, and says why in words, with one of these codes:

| Code              | The address                                 |
| ----------------- | ------------------------------------------- |
| `notHttps`        | isn't HTTPS                                 |
| `credentials`     | carries credentials                         |
| `queryOrFragment` | carries a query or a fragment               |
| `tooLong`         | is too long                                 |
| `malformed`       | isn't an address                            |
| `notPublic`       | has a host that isn't public                |
| `unknownHost`     | has a host that doesn't resolve             |
| `fixedAddress`    | isn't ElevenLabs', fal's or Shotstack's own |

A server whose operator set no keys to encrypt secrets keeps no key (`AssistantSecretsNotConfigured`).

</AccordionItem>
</Accordion>

### Choose a model for each use [#models]

Under **Models**, each use is a row: **Text**, for the writing assistant and the agent, **Images**, for the images made into the media library, **Video**, for the [videos](/guides/media#generate-video) made there, **Voice**, for the [voice-overs](/guides/media#voiceover), **Transcription**, for the [transcripts](/guides/media#transcribe) of its audio and videos, and **Slide videos**, for the [slide videos](/guides/media#make-a-slide-video) Shotstack composes, which takes no model. At its end, pick one of the connected providers that offers it, then its model. Peal offers the provider's current models; **Another model…** opens a field for one it doesn't list, saved when you leave it or press Enter. **Another provider** has no list, so you type its model. Each change applies at once.

Pick **This server's model** to leave a use to the instance's model, within the workspace's allowance, or **Off** when the instance has none. The provider stays connected.

Images come from OpenAI, Google Gemini, xAI, Together AI, fal (its FLUX models) or **Another provider** only. Google Gemini and xAI take the image's shape as a ratio (`1:1`, `3:2`, `2:3`), fal as `1:1`, `4:3` or `3:4`, the others as a size (`1024x1024`, `1536x1024`, `1024x1536`).

Videos come from Google Gemini (its Veo models) or xAI (its Grok Imagine models) only, with the workspace's key: the instance has no video model, so **Video** offers no **This server's model**. Peal bills nothing for videos; your account at the provider pays for each one. A workspace makes one video at a time and asks for 20 a day at most, each taking minutes. The member who asked gets a notice in the app when it's ready or failed. A video the server was making when it stopped fails as `interrupted` and is never asked for again, since the provider may already have billed it.

Voice-overs come from OpenAI, Google Gemini, Mistral, xAI, ElevenLabs or fal, and transcripts from OpenAI, Google Gemini, Mistral, xAI, Groq, ElevenLabs or fal, with the workspace's key only: the instance has none, so neither row offers **This server's model**. Each makes 50 a day at most. Peal bills nothing for them; your account at the provider pays.

### Slide videos with Shotstack [#shotstack]

Slide videos are composed by [Shotstack](https://shotstack.io), with your Shotstack account's key: Peal renders nothing itself. Connect **Shotstack** in **AI providers** with its key, then pick it in the **Slide videos** row, which has no model to choose. Your Shotstack account pays for each render at its prices, and Peal bills nothing for it.

- **Production**, the default, renders with your account's credits and no watermark. Shotstack gives a new account 10 free credits, one per minute of video, valid for 30 days.
- **Sandbox**, under **Advanced**, is free and watermarks every video. It takes your account's sandbox key, not the production one, and Shotstack asks for at least one credit in the account to use it.

On Peal Cloud, slide videos are for workspaces whose billing account has a payment method: on the free tier, **Make a slide video** opens the upgrade dialog instead. An instance that doesn't bill has no such limit. A workspace makes one slide video at a time and 20 a day at most. The [command line](/developers/cli/posts#media-slideshow) connects it with `peal assistant providers connect shotstack [--sandbox]`.

Once a use is on your provider, what members send for it goes there with your key, under that provider's terms. The instance's model is never asked instead, even when your provider refuses. What to expect:

- Text requests use none of the workspace's assistant requests, up to 500 a day, and at most four run at the same time: a fifth one sent meanwhile is refused (`TextProviderBusy`) and doesn't count. Each request counts, whatever it ends with.
- Images use none of the workspace's assistant requests, the free plan and a trial included, up to 50 a day. They still take room in the plan's storage.
- The model for text must give structured output (an answer that follows a JSON schema), or every request fails.
- When your provider refuses the key, the account, the model or the address, nothing is made, the page says so, and none of the instance's allowance is spent.

The [command line](/developers/cli/posts#assistant) has `peal assistant models`.

### Who can set the providers [#providers-access]

Only these can connect providers and choose their models:

- a member, in their own session;
- Peal's [command line](/developers/cli/posts#assistant) (`peal assistant providers` and `peal assistant models`);
- an [API key](#api-keys) or a [partner key](/developers/embedding#account) given `assistant:configure`.

No other application connected over OAuth holds that permission, an [MCP](/developers/mcp) client included, whatever it was granted. So none of them can send the workspace's drafts to an address of its own. Staff acting as a member can't set the providers or ask them anything, because the workspace's account at the provider would pay.

### Read the activity log [#activity]

Each workspace keeps a log of who changed what, and through which application. It records:

- every request that asked to change something in the workspace, whether it was done or refused;
- every call of an [MCP](/developers/mcp) tool that changes something.

Requests that only read aren't kept, and nor are those that check and make nothing, such as validating a post or importing a file as a dry run. A request that makes several posts at once (`posts.bulk`, `posts.import`) is one event, whatever it made.

Only owners can read the log. It needs the permission `activity:read`, which only the owner role holds. An owner can give that permission to an [API key](#api-keys), and an application an owner connected reads the log as they do.

The log has no page in the web app yet. You can read it in three ways:

- through the API, with `GET /api/v1/activity`, newest first, a page at a time ([Paging](/developers/conventions/paging#paging) shows an answer);
- from the [command line](/developers/cli/workspace#activity), with `peal activity list`;
- from an MCP client, with the tool `list_activity`.

To narrow it, `operation` keeps the events of one operation, such as `posts.schedule`. `actor` keeps those one member asked for, by their user id, or one API key, by its id.

Each event has these fields:

| Field          | What it holds                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                            |
| -------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| `occurredAt`   | When.                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                    |
| `operation`    | The endpoint, as the reference names it (`posts.schedule`, `channels.disconnect`). For an MCP tool, `mcp.` followed by its name (`mcp.create_draft`). For what the web app's settings change through the sign-in service (a role, a member removed, an invitation, the subscription), `auth:` followed by its path (`auth:/organization/remove-member`). |
| `status`       | The HTTP status answered. `null` for an MCP tool call, which has none.                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                   |
| `errorCode`    | The [error code](/developers/conventions/errors#error-codes) when the request was refused or failed, `null` when it was done. For an MCP tool, `tool_refused` when the tool answered an error, and `tool_failed` when the call ended without an answer.                                                                                                  |
| `actor`        | Who asked: a member (`user`, with their `userId`) or a key (`apiKey`, with its `apiKeyId`). A key is an API key of the workspace, a [partner key](/developers/embedding), or a [connect link](/guides/channels#connect-links), as `connect-link:` followed by its id. `via` tells which. Whoever opened a connect link is never named.                   |
| `via`          | How they authenticated: `session` (the web app), `apiKey`, `oauth` (an application through the API, such as the command line), `mcp`, `partnerKey` (a partner key, in a workspace its partner account owns) or `connectLink` (an account connected on a connect link's page).                                                                            |
| `application`  | The key or the application it came through, with its `id` and the `name` it had then. `null` for a person in the web app.                                                                                                                                                                                                                                |
| `impersonated` | Whether staff acting as the member asked. Never who.                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                     |
| `objectIds`    | The ids the request's path named, such as `postId` or `channelId`.                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                       |
| `requestId`    | The request's id, as its `X-Request-Id` header gave it ([Requests and answers](/developers/conventions#requests)).                                                                                                                                                                                                                                       |

The log holds nothing more. It never keeps what a request or its answer carried, a post's text, an address or a browser.

An event is kept {{keep-activity-en}}, then a daily job deletes it. Deleting the workspace deletes its log. When a member closes their account, the events they left in the remaining workspaces stay, without their id. Each member's [export](/guides/workspace/account#export) holds the changes they asked for themselves.

### Run a partner account [#partner]

A partner account lets a product of yours use Peal for its own customers. Under **Partner**, owners and admins manage the workspace's partner account. They set:

- what those customers see on the hosted pages: a name, a **Logo address** and an **Accent colour**;
- the **Return origins** those pages may send them back to;
- the partner keys, each shown once, right after it's created.

The page lists the newest workspaces the account owns, with their channels. Whether you can open an account yourself is the instance's choice. If you can't, the page tells you to ask the people who run it.

An account closes only once it owns no workspace. Staff acting as a member can't change it. [Embedding Peal](/developers/embedding) walks through the whole flow.

### Delete a workspace [#delete-workspace]

On **Workspace**, under **Danger zone**, owners click **Delete this workspace…**, then type the workspace's name to confirm.

<Callout type="danger" title="Deleting a workspace can't be undone">
  Everything in it goes for good: its channels and their authorizations, its
  posts, scheduled or not, and their analytics, its media library and the files
  in it, its notifications, its writing assistant usage, what Peal recorded of
  its subscriptions and of its posts on X, its members and invitations, and its
  API keys, which stop working at once. Nothing more is posted from it. Stripe
  keeps the invoices it issued.
</Callout>

Its channels go as when you disconnect them by hand: the platforms that allow it are asked to forget Peal, unless another workspace still connects the same account.

If one of the workspace's posts is going out, deleting waits. Try again a few minutes later.

The workspace may have a subscription Stripe can still bill: active, unpaid or paused, including one whose checkout has just completed before Stripe told Peal about it. The dialog then says first what deleting does to it, and waits until you tick **Cancel the subscription now**. Here's what happens to the subscription:

- It's cancelled at Stripe at once, and nothing more is charged.
- The period already paid for isn't refunded. There's no proration credit and no final invoice, except where the law gives a refund.
- The workspace's **Billing** page goes with it, so download your invoices from the billing portal first. Stripe keeps the invoices it issued, as accounting law requires (10 years in France), and the confirmation email says whom to ask for a copy.

The law's exception is for consumers. If you subscribed as a consumer less than 14 days ago, you can withdraw and get the unused part refunded. Withdraw first, with **Withdraw from contract here** on the workspace's billing page (see [Plans and billing](/guides/workspace/billing)). Once the workspace is deleted, ask whoever runs the server instead.

To stop paying and keep the workspace, cancel the subscription from its **Billing** page instead. It then ends with the period paid for.

<Accordion>
<AccordionItem title="Open checkouts, outages and several workspaces">

- A checkout of the workspace that's still open, in another tab say, is closed at Stripe as the workspace goes, so it can't be paid afterwards.
- When Stripe can't be reached, nothing is deleted. Try again a few minutes later. A subscription cancelled before the failure stays cancelled, and trying again cancels nothing more.
- Closing an account that deletes several subscribed workspaces lists every subscription it cancels, each with its workspace's billing page, before you tick the box.

</AccordionItem>

</Accordion>

Once the workspace is deleted, Peal sends a confirmation to your address, in your language.

## Your workspaces [#your-workspaces]

When you sign in or go to Peal's address, you land in the workspace you last created or switched to, or else in your first one. When you sign up, a page of its own asks you to name your first workspace and choose its address. It fills in both for you (_Ada’s workspace_, at `/adas-workspace`), and you can change either, as long as no other workspace has that address. If an invitation is waiting for you, you join that workspace instead. You can change the name and the address later, on the **Workspace** page under **Settings**.

To create another workspace, open the workspace menu at the top of the sidebar and click **New workspace**, which opens the same page with the name empty. **Back**, at its top, returns to your workspace. A workspace's pages live at an address made from its name, such as `/adas-workspace/posts`. The address stays the same when you rename the workspace, and links that hold the workspace's id, as Peal's emails do, lead to the same page.

Your account and your notifications are yours in every workspace: they're under **Settings** in each one, in the **Personal** group ([Account](/guides/workspace/account), [Notifications](/guides/workspace/notifications)).

Each workspace keeps its own posts, channels, inbox and analytics, and you see them from inside that workspace: no page, endpoint, command or MCP tool gathers them across workspaces. To see another workspace, switch to it in the workspace menu. Your [notifications](/guides/workspace/notifications) and the [weekly digest](/guides/workspace/notifications#digest) are the only places that cover all your workspaces at once.

## Through the API [#api]

These endpoints handle your workspace and your account:

| Endpoint                                 | What it does                                     |
| ---------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------ |
| `DELETE /api/v1/workspace`               | Deletes the workspace, given its `name` (owners) |
| `GET /api/me/export`                     | Answers your data as a JSON file                 |
| `DELETE /api/me`                         | Deletes your account, given its `password`       |
| `GET /api/me/connected-apps`             | Lists your connected applications                |
| `DELETE /api/me/connected-apps/{client}` | Revokes one                                      |

They answer only you, signed in to the web app with your own session. API keys, OAuth tokens and staff acting as you get 403. Your data and the list of your connected applications also answer the [command line](/developers/cli/workspace#account) (`peal account`), which you sign in at your own terminal. They never answer an MCP client or another OAuth client.

These endpoints handle members and invitations:

| Endpoint                                  | What it does                                                      |
| ----------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------- |
| `GET /api/v1/members`                     | Lists the members, to every member                                |
| `GET /api/v1/invitations`                 | Lists the pending invitations, to those who may invite            |
| `POST /api/v1/invitations`                | Invites an `email` with a `role`, as **Send the invitation** does |
| `DELETE /api/v1/invitations/{invitation}` | Revokes a pending invitation                                      |

They answer members in person, through the web app, the command line or an MCP client allowed to. They never answer an API key or staff acting as you.

Renaming a workspace, changing a member's role and removing a member go through the sign-in service's routes under `/api/auth/organization/`. API keys go through `/api/v1/api-keys`.
