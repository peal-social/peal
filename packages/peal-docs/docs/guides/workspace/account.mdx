Your account is yours, across every workspace you belong to. Its settings apply in all of them.

## Your settings [#account]

Open the account menu and click **Account settings**, or click **Account** in the sidebar of your own pages. The page has these settings:

| Setting                    | What it does                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                        |
| -------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Name**                   | The name other members see, and that your invitations sign with.                                                                                                                                                                                                    |
| **Language**               | English or French. Peal writes your emails in it, switches the app to it as soon as you save, and opens in it whenever you sign in.                                                                                                                                 |
| **Password**               | Your current password confirms it's you. Changing it signs your other sessions out, unless you untick **Sign out of my other sessions**.                                                                                                                            |
| **Time format**            | How this browser writes times of day: automatic, as your language writes them, or a 12-hour or 24-hour clock. The choice stays in this browser, and your other devices keep their own.                                                                              |
| **Sessions**               | The browsers and devices signed in to your account, with when they signed in and when they were last active. You can sign one out, or every session but the one you're using.                                                                                       |
| **Connected applications** | The [command line](/developers/cli) and the [MCP clients](/developers/mcp) you let act for you, with what they may do and in which workspaces. Click **Revoke** on one, and it can't renew its access any more. The access it already holds ends within 15 minutes. |

### Sign in with Google or your organisation's provider [#providers]

Where the server offers it, the sign-in and sign-up pages show **Continue with Google**, or **Continue with** followed by the name of the provider your server's operator set up. Peal's own service offers Google.

Here's what the button does on each page:

- On the sign-up page, it creates your account with the address your provider confirmed, and accepts the terms as the form does.
- On the sign-in page, it signs in the account that uses the same address, once both your provider and Peal have confirmed it. An account whose address Peal never confirmed has to sign in with its password first.

Two cases are refused:

- An account with two-step verification signs in with its password and its code. The provider's sign-in is refused for it.
- With Google, only a Gmail address or an address on a domain that Google Workspace manages is accepted. For any other address, sign in with your password, because Google can't confirm the address is still yours.

An account created this way has no password. Under **Set a password**, click **Email me a link** to get one at your address. The link works for an hour, and you can then sign in either way. Two-step verification and deleting your account both need a password first.

### Two-step verification [#two-step]

With two-step verification on, signing in asks for a code from an authenticator app on your phone, after your password. Your email address has to be confirmed first.

<Steps>
  <Step title="Turn it on">

Under **Two-step verification**, click **Turn on** and type your password.

  </Step>
  <Step title="Scan the QR code">

Scan the QR code Peal shows with the app, or type in its key, which Peal shows as text. Then type the 6-digit code the app shows.

  </Step>
  <Step title="Keep the recovery codes">

Peal then shows ten recovery codes, once. Copy or download them, and keep them somewhere safe. Each one works once, in place of a code, when you don't have your phone.

  </Step>
</Steps>

Turning it on doesn't sign out the browsers and applications already signed in. Sign them out from **Sessions** if you want to.

When you sign in, type the app's code after your password, or click **Use a recovery code**. A few rules apply:

- Select **Trust this browser for 30 days** to skip the second step on that browser until then. Turning two-step verification off forgets every trusted browser.
- After five wrong codes, you have to sign in with your password again. After ten in a row, two-step verification is locked for 15 minutes.
- The second step expires 10 minutes after the password.

The section says how many recovery codes you have left. From there, you can:

- Click **New recovery codes** to replace them all. It asks for your password.
- Click **Turn off**, which asks for your password too. It's refused while a workspace you belong to [requires it](/guides/workspace#require-two-step), and the section names those workspaces.

Peal emails your address each time two-step verification is turned on or off, new codes are made, or a recovery code is used to sign in. If it wasn't you, change your password.

If you lose your phone and your recovery codes, write to whoever runs the server. Once their staff know it's you, they can [reset it](/self-hosting/staff#actions), which signs you out everywhere. Sign in with your password, then turn it on again.

Peal doesn't offer codes by email or text message. An emailed code protects nothing when the mailbox is what was taken.

### Download your data [#export]

Click **Download my data** to save a JSON file of everything Peal keeps about you. It holds:

- your account, how you sign in, and your sessions, with their browser and address (but not the sessions staff opened to act as you, which are theirs);
- your memberships, and the invitations you sent or received;
- your connected applications, your notifications and your email preferences, including whether you hear about every post;
- the changes you asked for in workspaces, as their [activity logs](/guides/workspace#activity) keep them: when, which operation, how you signed in and the status answered, never what a request carried;
- what the server's staff did to your account (a ban, a confirmation sent again, acting as you, and each change they made while acting as you, with where and on which ids…), when, and the reason they gave, but not who did it;
- the posts you wrote in the workspaces you share, with where they went, the options each platform got, and whether YouTube kept a video private;
- the workspaces where you're the only member, in full (see below);
- what the law has the server keep beyond your account (`legalHold`).

For a workspace where you're the only member, the file holds its channels, posts, [tags, signatures, channel sets and default options](/guides/posts/organise), [queues](/guides/posts/queues#queues), analytics (each post's counts, as Peal read them), media, API keys, writing assistant usage and billing. Billing means its plan, how each of its subscriptions stands, and how many posts it made each month on X, with and without a link. Each image or video in the store comes with a link that downloads it for 7 days.

`legalHold` lists each connection that created, changed or deleted content in the past year: when, from which address, over which protocol, and how it signed in. It also says what closing your account would leave, for how long, why, and who may obtain it. On a server that keeps nothing for the law, it's `null`.

The rest of a workspace you share belongs to its team. The file holds your membership of it and the posts you wrote there, not the others' posts, its channels, its media or its analytics. It holds no password, token, key or card. Payment details and invoices stay with Stripe, in the billing portal.

Each time a copy is downloaded, from the web app or the [command line](/developers/cli/workspace#account), Peal emails your address to say so. If it wasn't you, change your password and sign out your other sessions.

### Delete your account [#delete-account]

Click **Delete my account…** and type your password. The account is then deleted for good. As at sign-in, after three passwords tried within 10 seconds, you'll need to wait a few seconds before trying another.

<Callout type="danger" title="Deleting your account can't be undone">
  The workspaces where you're the only member go with everything in them, as
  [deleting a workspace](/guides/workspace#delete-workspace) does.
</Callout>

Here's what else happens:

- You leave the other workspaces. The posts you wrote there stay, without your name, and so do the channels you connected, which belong to the workspace.
- Connections you started and didn't finish go too, with your sessions, notifications, preferences, invitations and connected applications.
- Peal sends a confirmation to your address, in your language.

While you're the only owner of a workspace that other members use, you can't delete the account, and Peal lists those workspaces. Make another member an owner of each one, or delete them, first.

A workspace your account would delete may have a subscription Stripe can still bill. The dialog then says what that ends, and waits until you tick **Cancel the subscription now**, as [deleting a workspace](/guides/workspace#delete-workspace) does. Those subscriptions are cancelled at once, without a refund of the period paid for, and the confirmation email says so.

#### What the law keeps [#legal-hold]

French law has a host keep a few things after an account is closed. On Peal Cloud, and on any server whose operator that law binds, only those are left. They're kept apart from everything else, and read only to answer a judicial authority's written request:

| What is kept                                                                                       | For how long                                    |
| -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------- |
| Your name and email address                                                                        | {{keep-legal-identity-en}}                      |
| How you signed in (each sign-in method's identifier, never your password) and your Stripe customer | {{keep-legal-account-en}}                       |
| Each connection that created content                                                               | {{keep-legal-connections-en}} after it was made |

A daily job then deletes them. The dialog and the confirmation say so when the server keeps them. On any other server, nothing is left ([Self-hosting](/self-hosting/privacy#legal-retention) says which operators have to keep them).
