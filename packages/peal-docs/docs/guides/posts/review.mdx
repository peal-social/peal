An agency shows a post to its client before it's published. A team has a manager look at what a contributor wrote. Peal has two tools for this:

- A **review link** shows one post to someone without an account, who can comment on it and approve it.
- A workspace can make posts wait for an **approval** before they're scheduled.

## In short, for an agency [#agency]

Here's the whole round trip, from turning approvals on to the post going out:

<Steps>
<Step title="Turn approvals on">

An owner or an admin clicks **Settings** at the foot of the workspace's sidebar and, on **Workspace**, sets **Approvals** to **Always**, or to **When a contributor or an application asks**.

</Step>
<Step title="Ask for approval">

Write the post and click **Ask for approval** (the button that otherwise says **Schedule**). The post has its time and shows on the calendar as **Awaiting approval**, but it doesn't go out.

</Step>
<Step title="Send a review link">

On the post's page, under **Review links**, create a link that lets whoever opens it **View, comment and approve**. The link is shown only once, so copy it then, and send it to your client.

</Step>
<Step title="Your client decides">

Your client opens the link, without an account, and sees the post as each channel will receive it. They can comment, then click **Approve this post** or **Request changes**.

</Step>
<Step title="The post goes out, or comes back">

Once approved, the post is scheduled for its time. If your client requested changes, it's a draft again, with their comment. Correct it and ask again.

</Step>
</Steps>

Members who can approve posts can also approve them in Peal, on the post's page or from the list of posts that wait: click **Posts** in the sidebar, then the **Needs approval** tab, or press G then A.

## Choose which posts wait [#policy]

Each workspace has one approval policy, under **Approvals** on the **Workspace** page that opens when you click **Settings** at the foot of the sidebar. Owners and admins choose it from three:

| Policy                                        | What waits for an approval                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                   |
| --------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Never** (until you change it)               | Nothing. Whoever can schedule posts does, and contributors write drafts.                                                                                                                                                                                                                     |
| **When a contributor or an application asks** | What a contributor asks to send, and what's scheduled with an [API key](/guides/workspace#api-keys) or through an application signed in for a member: the [command line](/developers/cli) or an [AI assistant](/developers/mcp). Owners, admins and editors schedule at once in the web app. |
| **Always**                                    | Every post, whoever asks.                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                    |

With a policy other than **Never**, contributors can ask for a post to go out, which they otherwise can't. Viewers still can't.

The policy applies to posts scheduled from then on. A post that's already scheduled stays scheduled, and a post that waits keeps waiting.

Every way of scheduling goes through the same check: the web app, the [API](/developers), an API key, the command line and AI assistants. None of them gets round it.

## A post that waits [#waiting]

A post that waits for an approval has the status **Awaiting approval**. It has its time and its channels, and it shows on the [calendar](/guides/posts/schedule#calendar) at that time. It also counts in the month's posts of your [plan](/guides/workspace/billing). Nothing publishes it until it's approved.

Like a scheduled post, it keeps its text locked. To change it, click **Back to draft**, which withdraws the request.

Whoever asked, and whoever can schedule, can move it to another time, take it back to draft or cancel it. Where the policy lets a member schedule without an approval, that member can also schedule it themselves, which withdraws the request.

### Approve [#approve]

Owners, admins and editors can approve a post on its page (**Approve**), or with the API, the command line or an AI assistant. Someone without an account can approve through a review link that allows it.

On **Posts**, a post that waits has **Approve** and **Request changes** at the end of its row. To decide on several at once, select them, click **Actions** and choose **Approve** (or press V) or **Request changes** (R). Peal asks before it approves, as an approval can't be taken back, and asks for one comment when you request changes. A post of the selection that doesn't wait is left alone, and the action says how many it reaches, as in **Approve (2 of 3)**.

Peal records each decision: who decided (for a link, the name they gave and the link they used), what, and when. The post's page shows this history.

An approval is for what its reviewer read. If the post was changed and asked again while their page was open, their click approves nothing. Peal tells them the post changed and shows it again, so they decide on what it says now. The same holds for a request for changes.

Whoever can approve can approve a post they wrote or asked to send. An approval says that someone allowed to has looked, and doesn't say that two people have. If you need a second person, have contributors write and editors approve.

Applications follow these rules:

- An application doesn't approve what an application asked for. What an API key, the command line or an AI assistant scheduled waits for a person: a member in the web app, or a reviewer through a link a member created there.
- For the same reason, a link that lets its holders approve is created in the web app only, never with an API key, the command line or an AI assistant.
- An application can approve what a member asked for in the web app, if you let it schedule and publish (`workspace:publish`). Approving a post is what sends it out, so an application that only writes drafts approves nothing.

<Accordion>
<AccordionItem title="When the workspace is over its plan">

Approving a post is what schedules it. While the post waited, the workspace may have come to hold more channels or members than its [plan](/guides/workspace/billing) allows, as when a trial ends. The approval then schedules nothing.

The approval is refused and the reviewer is told so. Someone who came through a link is told only that the team can't schedule posts at the moment. The post keeps waiting.

Its author isn't told again. The post's page still shows it awaiting approval, and the billing page shows what the plan allows. Once the workspace is back within its plan, the post is approved as usual.

</AccordionItem>
</Accordion>

### Request changes [#changes]

**Request changes** sends the post back to draft with a comment that says what to change. Its author and whoever asked are told. Once it's corrected, ask again, which starts a new request.

### When the approval comes late [#late]

An approval that comes after the post's time doesn't publish it late. The post goes back to draft, still approved, and its author is told to give it a new time. Scheduling it again needs no new approval as long as it says the same thing. A time less than a minute ago still counts as now.

### What an approval covers [#covers]

An approval is for what the post says and where it goes. Any of these changes voids it, and the post waits again the next time it's scheduled:

- its text, or the text it has on one channel
- its images and videos: which ones, their order and their alt text, and the ones it shows on one channel instead of its own
- the channels it goes to
- its [platform options](/guides/posts#options) on a channel, such as a YouTube title

Its time isn't part of it. Those who can schedule can move an approved post without a new approval.

## Review links [#links]

A review link shows one post to whoever holds it. They don't need an account, and they don't sign in.

On the post's page, under **Review links**, members who can approve posts create a link and choose two things:

| Choice            | Values                                                                |
| ----------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| What it allows    | **View only**, **View and comment**, or **View, comment and approve** |
| How long it works | 14 days unless you choose otherwise, 90 days at most                  |

Peal shows the link once, when it's created, and keeps no copy of it, only a fingerprint that recognises it. If you lose it, create another. A post has at most 20 working links.

<Callout type="warning" title="Anyone who has the link can open it">
  Send it only to the people who should see the post. If it went to the wrong
  place, revoke it.
</Callout>

### What the reviewer sees [#link-page]

The page behind a link shows the post as each channel will receive it:

- the channel's name and platform
- the text the post has there
- what it has there besides its text (where a Pin leads and its board, a video's title and who sees it, a call to action's address)
- its images and videos with their alt text written out (on a channel that shows its own, those, under the channel)
- its time in the reader's own time zone

It's an honest preview, and doesn't imitate each platform's layout. It shows nothing else of your workspace: not its name, its members' addresses, its other posts or its channels.

### When a link stops working [#link-end]

A link stops working when it expires or you revoke it, and as soon as the post is published, cancelled or deleted. The page then says which. While a post is a draft again after changes were requested, its link still shows it.

You can create links for drafts, posts that wait and scheduled posts. Staff acting as a member can't create them.

## Comments [#comments]

Each post has comments, on its page in Peal and on its review link's page when the link allows comments. A comment starts a thread and others answer it. Members resolve a thread once it's dealt with, or reopen it.

A comment can be about the whole post, or about some words of the text on one channel. To comment on words, select them in the preview, then write. The comment keeps the words it quotes. If the text changes later, the comment still points at those words wherever they moved. Once the text no longer contains them, the comment keeps the quote and says the text has changed.

Who comments, and how:

- Members comment as themselves. Owners, admins, editors and contributors can comment and resolve, and viewers read.
- Someone who came through a link gives a name, shown with their comments and decisions. The workspace's members hear about their comments at most once an hour per link.
- Comments are plain text, 2000 characters at most. Nothing typed is read as formatting or as code, and control characters are removed.

The members who follow a post hear about its review through [notifications](/guides/workspace/notifications): its author, whoever asked for its approval, and whoever commented on it. Members who can approve hear when a post starts waiting.

## For reviewers without an account [#reviewers]

If someone sent you a review link, this is how it works:

- Open the whole link. Its end, after the `#`, is what opens the post, and your browser doesn't send that part to any other site.
- Give your name before you comment or decide. The team that sent you the link sees it with what you write.
- Peal asks for no email address and sends you nothing. To read the answers, open the link you were sent again.
- You may be asked to complete a check that you're not a robot before you send something.

Peal keeps the name you gave, with your comments and decisions, for 24 months. After that, they stay without a name. Everything is deleted earlier if the post or the workspace is deleted. To have it removed sooner, ask the team that sent you the link, or the operator of the Peal instance.

## Through the API [#api]

The [API](/developers) has the same operations, under `/api/v1`:

| Path                                                                                 | What it is for      |
| ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ | ------------------- |
| `approvals/settings`                                                                 | The policy          |
| `approvals`                                                                          | The posts that wait |
| `approvals/{postId}`, and `comments`, `approve`, `request-changes`, `links` under it | A post's review     |

A post that waits answers `"status": "awaitingApproval"` wherever posts are listed. Scheduling a post that needs an approval answers with the post, waiting, instead of an error, so check its `status`.

`approvals/{postId}` answers with the post as each channel will receive it, and the approval that waits. `approve` and `request-changes` take that approval's `id` (as `approvalId`) and its `subjectHash`, the fingerprint of what the post said then. If the post was asked again or says something else since, they answer `ApprovalChanged` (409) and decide nothing. Read `approvals/{postId}` again, then decide.

The [command line](/developers/cli/posts#review) has `peal approvals` and `peal review`. [AI assistants](/developers/mcp/tools#tools) have `list_approvals`, `get_post_review`, `approve_post` and `request_changes`.

## On your own server [#self-hosting]

Reviews work on every instance, with nothing to set. They have these limits and settings:

- Visitors' comments and decisions are limited to 20 every 10 minutes per address and 60 an hour per link, and requests to 16 KiB.
- When the instance has [Turnstile keys](/self-hosting/environment-variables), visitors complete its check before they write. The check loads only once a visitor starts to write, so reading a post through a link contacts no one but your instance and its media store.
- A link's address is `PUBLIC_URL/review#<token>`.
