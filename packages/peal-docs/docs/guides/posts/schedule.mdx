Schedule a post once its text and channels are ready ([Write a post](/guides/posts)).

## Set the time [#schedule]

<Tabs>
<Tab title="Web app">
<Steps>
<Step title="Pick the date and time">

Under **When**, set the **Date**, **Time** and **Time zone**. The time zone starts as your browser's.

</Step>
<Step title="Schedule it">

Click **Schedule**. If you can't, the post breaks a limit on one of its channels, and the composer lists what to fix. Once you click it, the composer also lists any [platform options](/guides/posts#options) a channel still lacks, and shows them under their fields.

</Step>
</Steps>

</Tab>
<Tab title="CLI">

```sh
peal posts schedule <post-id> --at 2026-11-20T10:00 --time-zone Europe/Paris
```

See [Posts on the command line](/developers/cli/posts#posts).

</Tab>
</Tabs>

Peal reads the time as a clock in that time zone shows it, so a post set for 09:00 goes out at 09:00 there all year. Two nights a year need a rule:

- When the clocks go forward, a time they skip, such as 02:30, becomes the moment they jump to, 03:00.
- When they go back, a time that happens twice means the first one. The post goes out once.

A time less than a minute ago counts as now. Anything earlier is refused, so pick a time that's still ahead.

### Publish now [#publish-now]

To send a post at once, click **Publish now**. You'll find it next to **Schedule** on a draft, and next to **Move to this time** on a scheduled post.

It schedules the post for the moment you confirm, in your browser's time zone, so the post goes through the same checks as with **Schedule**.

<Callout
  type="warning"
  title="A published post can't be taken back from the composer"
>
  **Publish now** asks you to confirm first, and names the channels the post
  goes to. A channel that is turned off or needs reconnecting is listed apart,
  because the post can't go out there.
</Callout>

**Publish now** isn't offered when your posts need an approval, or while the composer is still finding out whether they do. An approval that came after the post's time would publish nothing.

## Move, unschedule or cancel it [#move-cancel]

Once a post is scheduled, its text is locked. You can still change its time, take it back to draft to edit it, or cancel it.

### Move it [#move]

In the composer, change the date, time or time zone, then click **Move to this time**. On the calendar, you can also drag the post by its handle to another hour or day. It keeps the time zone it was scheduled in.

Moving a post doesn't check its text or media against its channels again.

### Edit its text [#back-to-draft]

Click **Back to draft**. The post leaves the calendar and has no time any more. Edit it, then click **Schedule** again, which checks it against its channels once more.

### Cancel it [#cancel]

To cancel the whole post, click **Cancel the post** in the composer and confirm. It goes out on no channel, and you can't schedule it again.

To keep it off one channel only, click **Remove** next to that channel under **Channels it goes to**. The post still goes out on the others at its time, and that channel shows **Taken off the post**. You can't remove the last channel: cancel the post instead.

Removing a channel counts as an edit:

- If your posts need an [approval](/guides/posts/review#policy), the approval the post had no longer covers it, and it waits for a new one.
- If the post goes back to draft (by you, a reviewer, or an approval that came too late), it keeps its other channels. The removed channel loses its own text and images, and starts afresh if you tick it again.

### On the command line [#move-cancel-cli]

```sh
peal posts reschedule <post-id> --at 2026-11-21T10:00 --time-zone Europe/Paris
peal posts unschedule <post-id>
peal posts cancel <post-id>
peal posts remove-target <post-id> <target-id>
```

## The calendar [#calendar]

In a workspace, click **Calendar** in the sidebar. The calendar shows scheduled posts by **Day**, **Week** or **Month**, at their times in your browser's time zone, with each post's status. The line beside the dates names that zone.

From the calendar, you can:

- Click a post to open it in the composer.
- Drag a post to another hour or day ([Move it](#move)).
- Click **New post** on a day, or on an hour of the week that hasn't started, to write a post for that time.
- Filter the posts by [tag](/guides/posts/organise#tags) and by channel, above the calendar.

The day view has a row for each time that holds a post or a free slot in a [queue](/guides/posts/queues#queues), with the queue's name. A slot taken by a post your filters hide says so, and doesn't offer **New post**. On the days the clocks change, each time shows its offset.

In the month view, a day with more posts than fit opens in the day view.

Times follow your language's clock. To switch between a 12-hour and a 24-hour clock, set **Time format** on your account page. The choice is kept in your browser.

To open the calendar on a given week, add `?date=` and any day of that week to its address, such as `?date=2026-11-09`.

## When a post comes due [#publishing]

At its time, Peal sends the post to each of its channels separately, one post at a time per account. It records how each one ended:

| On a channel | What happened                                                                                             |
| ------------ | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Published    | The platform took the post, and gave its address if it has one.                                           |
| Failed       | The platform refused the post, the account needs reconnecting, or the post went out too late (see below). |
| To check     | Peal can't tell whether the platform published it. Check the platform before posting again.               |
| Cancelled    | The post was cancelled before it went out there.                                                          |

The post as a whole then shows **Published**, **Partly published**, **To check**, **Failed** or **Cancelled**. Its author hears how it went on each channel, and so do the members who asked to hear about every post ([Notifications](/guides/workspace/notifications)).

## Follow up on a post [#follow-up]

Open a post that went out, from the calendar or from a notification. Its page shows the text and, for each channel:

- its status, and why it failed or needs checking;
- the post's address on the platform;
- every attempt Peal made, and when it tried again.

The page also says how the post was created and scheduled: in the web app, with an API key, or by a named application such as the command line or an MCP client. While the post is going out, the page updates by itself.

If you can schedule posts, you can then:

- Click **Post again** on a channel where the post failed or needs checking. Peal sends it now, as a new send with its own attempts, and the post shows **Publishing** until it's gone. If it needs checking, look on the platform first: the post may already be live.
- Click **Say what you found** on a channel to check, then **It is live** (with the post's address, if you have it) or **It is not there**, which marks it failed so you can post it again. Either answer takes it off the posts to check and marks its notifications as read.

### Retries [#retries]

When a platform is busy, Peal waits and tries again:

- A platform that doesn't answer is tried again after 1, 5, 15, 60 and 60 minutes. After that, the post fails there.
- A platform that limits how often Peal can post is tried again when it says, up to 8 times, and no later than a day after the post's time.
- A platform that no longer accepts the account's access fails the post there, and the channel needs [reconnecting](/guides/channels#reconnect).

Peal sends each image or video in the form the platform needs, and some platforms process videos first. Peal waits up to 15 minutes for them, then tries again later, as for a platform that doesn't answer.

<Callout
  type="warning"
  title="A post that can't start within an hour of its time doesn't go out"
>
  This happens when the server was down at the post's time, for example. The
  post is marked missed.
</Callout>

<Accordion>
<AccordionItem title="When a platform doesn't answer the publishing request">

Peal checks only in ways that can't post twice:

| Platform                | How Peal checks                                                                                                                  |
| ----------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Bluesky                 | Reads the post back.                                                                                                             |
| Mastodon                | Sends it again under the same key, and Mastodon answers with the post it already made.                                           |
| X                       | Looks for it among the account's recent posts, by its text, links and files. It never sends it to X again.                       |
| Threads and Instagram   | Asks whether the post they were handed was published.                                                                            |
| Facebook Pages          | Looks for it among the Page's recent posts, by its text.                                                                         |
| TikTok                  | Follows the post TikTok already took, by the id TikTok gave.                                                                     |
| YouTube                 | Asks how far the upload got. It never uploads the video twice.                                                                   |
| Pinterest               | Looks for the Pin among those made on its board since, by title, description, link and kind of media. It never creates it again. |
| Google Business Profile | Looks for the post among the location's posts, by its type, text and event title.                                                |
| LinkedIn company page   | Looks for the post among the page's newest, by its text.                                                                         |
| LinkedIn profile        | Can't check: LinkedIn doesn't let Peal read a member's posts or send one twice safely, so the post shows **To check** at once.   |

Peal tries three times, over about two and a half minutes. If the server stops in the middle of a post, it checks the same way when it starts again. Only when none of that settles it does the post show **To check**. Peal never posts it again by itself.
