A post has one text, and each of its channels can replace it with its own. Once you schedule it, it keeps the time zone you scheduled it in. You write it in the composer, and you'll find it on the calendar afterwards.

## Who can do what [#roles]

What you can do with posts depends on your role in the workspace:

| Role        | Posts                                                         |
| ----------- | ------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Owner       | Write, schedule, move, cancel, repost and discard any draft   |
| Admin       | Write, schedule, move, cancel, repost and discard any draft   |
| Editor      | Write, schedule, move, cancel, repost and discard any draft   |
| Contributor | Write and save drafts, and discard their own. Can't schedule. |
| Viewer      | Read                                                          |

Cancelling a post (a draft included), taking it back to draft, reposting it and saying whether it went out all need the same right as scheduling it. A post's author is the member who created it. Every role but Viewer can also ask the [writing assistant](/guides/posts/assistant#assistant).

A workspace can make posts wait for an [approval](/guides/posts/review) before they're scheduled. Contributors can then ask for a post to go out, and a post that waits shows as **Awaiting approval**.

## Fill in the composer [#write]

To start, click **Posts** in a workspace's sidebar, then **New post**.

<Steps>
<Step title="Pick the channels">

Click **Channels**, beside the text, and tick the accounts the post goes to. The list holds the workspace's [channels](/guides/channels), grouped by platform, and you can type to search it by a name, a handle or a platform.

**All channels** ticks every channel that publishes, leaving out any that's turned off or needs reconnecting, and unticks them all once they're all ticked. A platform's line ticks all of its channels that publish (those your search shows). A channel set ticks its channels.

</Step>
<Step title="Write the text">

Type in **Text for all channels**. Mentions, hashtags and links stand out as you type, and they're kept as plain text.

</Step>
<Step title="Change the text on one channel, if you like">

Under **Each channel**, open the channel's row, click **Write its own** and write its text there. If you leave it empty, the channel uses the text for all channels.

</Step>
<Step title="Add images and videos">

Under **Images and videos**, attach images or videos from the media library, or upload them there. See [Images and videos](/guides/media).

</Step>
<Step title="Give one channel other images and videos, if you like">

In the channel's row, beside **Images and videos**, tick **Its own, instead of the post's**. The channel starts from the post's images and videos, and you can add, remove, reorder and describe them there.

The channel is then checked, counted and sent with those, while the other channels keep the post's. Untick the box to show the post's again. A channel needs a text to go out with images and videos of its own, either its own or the text for all channels.

</Step>
<Step title="Fill in the platform options">

When a channel's platform asks for more, such as a YouTube video's title, the channel's row says which fields it still needs. Open the row and fill them in. See [Platform options](#options).

When the post goes to several channels on one platform, a channel's row has a button that gives the others the same choices, such as **Use these options on the 2 other Instagram channels**. Each keeps what its account holds of its own, such as a Pinterest board. TikTok's options are set channel by channel.

</Step>
<Step title="Save the draft">

Click **Save as draft** to keep the post without a time. It joins the workspace's **Drafts**, which the calendar doesn't show.

</Step>
</Steps>

<Accordion>
<AccordionItem title="Right-to-left text, emoji and pasting">

Writing and pasting work like this:

- Each line takes the direction of its first letter, so a text in Arabic or Hebrew reads right to left.
- The smiley button in the toolbar under the text opens an emoji picker in your language. The emoji goes in where your cursor was, in the text you last wrote in (the text for all channels, a channel's own text or a part), and undo takes it back. The picker's data comes from Peal itself, never from another site.
- An image or a video pasted alone into a text is uploaded and attached as if you'd dropped it there (to its part, when you paste it into a part), with the same limits.
- A paste that also holds text, such as cells copied from a spreadsheet, which come with a picture of them, pastes the text only.

</AccordionItem>

</Accordion>

### Check the length on each channel [#length]

Under **Each channel**, each channel's row shows how much room its platform leaves, and anything else it would refuse, images and videos included. Platforms count differently, so the same text can fit on one and not on another:

| Platform                | Text limit                                                                                                                                                      | Images and videos       |
| ----------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------- |
| Bluesky                 | 300 characters, and 3,000 bytes                                                                                                                                 | 4                       |
| Mastodon                | 500 characters (the default of a server)                                                                                                                        | 4                       |
| X                       | 280, where a link counts 23, with or without `https://` (`peal.social` too), and an emoji or a character outside the Latin, Greek and Cyrillic scripts counts 2 | 4                       |
| Threads                 | 500 characters, where an emoji counts its bytes, 4 for most. 5 different links.                                                                                 | 20                      |
| LinkedIn                | 3,000 characters                                                                                                                                                | 20                      |
| Instagram               | 2,200 characters, 30 hashtags, 20 mentions. An image or a video is required.                                                                                    | 20                      |
| TikTok                  | 2,200, where most emoji count 2 (UTF-16 code units). An image or a video is required.                                                                           | One video, or 35 photos |
| YouTube                 | A description of 5,000 bytes, without `<` or `>`. A video is required.                                                                                          | 1                       |
| Pinterest               | A description of 800 characters. An image or a video is required.                                                                                               | One video, or 5 images  |
| Google Business Profile | 1,500 characters                                                                                                                                                | Photos only             |

<Callout type="info" title="Limits are checked when you schedule">
  You can save a draft that breaks a limit. Peal checks the limits when you
  schedule the post.
</Callout>

<Accordion>
<AccordionItem title="How some platforms count">

Some platforms count in their own way:

- Bluesky counts what a reader sees as one character, so an emoji made of several code points counts once.
- YouTube counts bytes, so an accented letter counts two.
- TikTok takes 2,200 for a video's caption and 4,000 for a photo post's description. Peal holds both to 2,200.
- Facebook's limit is the figure commonly reported, because Meta names none. So are Google Business Profile's 1,500 characters, and the 58 of an event's or an offer's title, because Google's reference and help pages name none.

</AccordionItem>

</Accordion>

### Preview it [#previews]

**Preview**, at the top of the composer, shows **Previews** beside the text, in place of the post's details, until you click it again. It shows what each channel the post goes to will receive:

- its name and platform
- the text it gets, as plain text, counted the way its platform counts it
- its images and videos, and the parts that follow

While they show, Peal asks for the previews a second after you stop typing, through the same check as the API's `posts.validate`. Each preview is the post as it would be saved, signatures and options included. It doesn't imitate the platform, which lays the post out its own way.

A channel that would refuse the post is marked, and its row under **Each channel** says what to fix. If short links are on, links show as you wrote them, because they're shortened only when the post goes out.

## Write a thread [#threads]

A post can have parts that follow it. To add the first, click **Add a part** in the toolbar under the text. **Thread and first comment** then shows below, with **Add a part** for the next ones. Each part has its own text, its own images and videos and a counter for every channel. It can also wait after the part before it, from a minute to a day. Use a part's buttons to move it up or down, or to remove it. A post has at most 24 parts after its first.

On the platforms with threads, each part goes out as a reply to the part before it:

| Platform | A post's parts become                                                |
| -------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| X        | A thread: each part replies to the one before it                     |
| Bluesky  | A thread: each part names the thread's first post and its own parent |
| Mastodon | A thread: each part replies to the one before it                     |

Each part follows the rules of a post on its platform, for its length (counted the way the platform counts it) and its images and videos. On Facebook Pages, Instagram, LinkedIn pages and YouTube, the one part after the post is a [first comment](#first-comment) instead.

The other platforms take a post alone. A post with parts is refused there when you schedule it, and the composer tells you as soon as you tick such a channel.

<Callout type="warning" title="Threads on X depend on the server's access">
  X documents that an app on self-serve access may reply only under a post whose
  author mentioned or quoted the replying account. If X refuses the second part,
  the post is partly published, with the reason that X doesn't allow this app to
  post replies, and you can't post it again from Peal. Whoever runs the server
  can turn X threads off ([`X_THREADS`](/self-hosting/environment-variables)),
  and X then takes a post alone.
</Callout>

The post's address is its first part's, and [analytics](/guides/engage/analytics) reads the counts of the first part.

### When a part doesn't go out [#thread-failures]

Parts go out in order, each as its own request to the platform. When one fails, or when you cancel, here's what happens:

| When                      | What happens                                                                                                              |
| ------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| A part failed             | Nothing is sent after it. The channel shows **Failed**, and its page lists the parts that are live, with their addresses. |
| Peal can't confirm a part | Nothing is sent after it. The channel shows **To check**. Look on the platform, then say what you found.                  |
| You click **Cancel**      | The parts not sent yet stop. A part that's waiting isn't sent once its wait ends, and the parts that went out stay out.   |

After a part failed, **Post again** sends that part and the ones after it, never a part that's live.

After a part to check turns out to be live and other parts were left, the channel shows **Failed** with the reason **Later parts not sent**. A part you found by hand has no id Peal knows, so the next part can't reply to it. Peal offers no **Post again** there, and the API refuses a repost (409 `target_status_conflict`). Post the rest on the platform yourself, as replies to the last part that's live. The same holds when the part to reply to is gone from the platform.

## Add a first comment [#first-comment]

On some platforms, the part after the post is a first comment. The account posts it under its own post once the post is live. People often use it for a link or hashtags they want to keep out of the post itself.

Write it as the post's second part, under **Thread and first comment**. The composer names each channel where it goes out as a first comment:

| Platform       | Where the first comment goes                                             |
| -------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| Facebook Pages | Under the Page's post, as the Page                                       |
| Instagram      | Under the post, as the account                                           |
| LinkedIn       | Under a company page's post, as the page. A member's profile takes none. |
| YouTube        | Under the video, as the channel                                          |

The comment goes out after the post, through the same channel, after the part's wait. An approval covers the first comment, as it covers every part.

### What a first comment can hold [#first-comment-limits]

A first comment is text alone. It takes no image or video, and a post has at most one. A story or a reel takes none.

Its length is held to what the platform allows a post's text, because none of these platforms says how long a comment may be. That limit isn't verified, so a platform may refuse a long comment that Peal let through, once the post is live. LinkedIn's help gives 1,250 characters for comments in groups, so keep a LinkedIn comment short.

Two platforms have rules of their own:

- YouTube turns comments off on a video made for kids. A post with a first comment and **Made for kids** is refused there when you schedule it.
- LinkedIn needs the page's administrator to have connected the page. LinkedIn lists no content administrator among the roles that comment, so the comment would be refused.

### Where first comments are offered [#first-comment-server]

A first comment uses the permission that answers comments in the [inbox](/guides/engage/inbox). So it's offered only where the server reads that platform's comments ([`PEAL_INBOX_PLATFORMS`](/self-hosting/environment-variables)). Elsewhere, the platform takes a post alone.

When the server stops reading a platform's comments, a post already scheduled there with a first comment is refused when its time comes, before anything goes out. The reason says first comments are turned off.

A channel connected before the server read its comments has to be connected again. Until then, a post with a first comment is refused there, and the channel's capabilities say so.

### When the comment fails [#first-comment-failures]

If the post is live and the comment fails, the post stays published. Here's what you'll see:

- The channel shows **Failed**, and the post is **partly published**.
- Webhooks get `post.finished` with that outcome.
- The notice says the post is live and only its first comment didn't go out.
- Analytics, the inbox and ads count the post as live all the same.

From there, click **Post again** to send the comment alone. Or click **Publish without the first comment** to give it up: the channel shows **Published**, like its post, and nothing more is sent.

None of these platforms keeps a key that would tell a comment sent twice apart. So when the platform's answer is lost, Peal looks for the comment among the post's newest comments, and takes the one the account wrote since the attempt began, with the same text. If it finds none, or two, the channel shows **To check**. Peal never sends the comment again blindly.

## Add a poll [#polls]

A post can ask a poll, and its text is the question. Click **Add a poll** in the toolbar under the text, then write two to four choices under **Poll**. Choose how long the poll stays open and whether readers can pick several choices.

Three platforms take polls from Peal:

| Platform | Choices                    | Open for                   | Several picks |
| -------- | -------------------------- | -------------------------- | ------------- |
| X        | 2 to 4, 25 characters each | 5 minutes to 7 days        | No            |
| Mastodon | 2 to 4, 50 characters each | 5 minutes to about 30 days | Yes           |
| Threads  | 2 to 4, 25 characters each | As long as Threads decides | No            |

A poll goes out with the post's first part. It never goes with an image or a video, because these platforms refuse both together. Mastodon's bounds are its defaults, so a server set otherwise may refuse the poll when the post goes out. Threads sets its own time, whatever the post asks.

The other platforms take no poll from Peal. A post with a poll is refused there, and the composer tells you. LinkedIn's polls have a question of their own, and Instagram's go through Facebook Login only, so Peal doesn't send either.

An approval covers the poll: its choices, how long it's open, and several picks.

## Platform options [#options]

Some platforms ask each post for more than its text and media. When the post goes to a channel on one of them, that channel's row under **Each channel** shows its fields, and names the required ones still empty. A draft keeps them as they are, unfinished. To schedule, you need the ones marked required, and each field shows what its platform would refuse.

Here are the options for each platform:

| Platform                | Options                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                 |
| ----------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| YouTube                 | **Title** (required: 100 characters at most, without `<` or `>`), **Made for kids** (required, with no default), **Visibility** (public unless you choose otherwise), **Tags** (500 characters in all), **Category number**, **Notify subscribers** (on), altered or synthetic content, and [more](/platforms/youtube#options)                          |
| TikTok                  | **Who can view this post** (required, with no default), **Allow comments**, **Allow Duet** and **Allow Stitch** (off until you turn them on), commercial content disclosure, AI-generated content, and [more](/platforms/tiktok#options)                                                                                                                |
| LinkedIn                | **Who can see this post** (anyone unless you choose otherwise; connections only is for a profile), **Keep others from resharing this post**, **Pages to mention** (company pages, each written `@Name` in the text), **Link to show as a card** with its **Link title** (required with a link) and **Link description**, **Document title** (for a PDF) |
| Pinterest               | **Board** (required, one of the account's), **Board section**, **Title** (100 characters at most), **Link** (where a click on the Pin leads, starting with `http://` or `https://`), **Video cover, at second** (the first frame unless you choose), **Dominant colour** (such as `#6E7874`)                                                            |
| Instagram               | A reel's settings and **Collaborators** (see [Instagram](/platforms/instagram#options))                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                 |
| Facebook                | A video's **Video title** (see [Facebook](/platforms/facebook#options))                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                 |
| Google Business Profile | **Post type** (an update unless you choose an event or an offer), **Title**, **Starts** and **Ends** (required for an event or an offer; a title of 58 characters at most), **Button** and its **Button link** (on an update or an event), **Coupon code**, **Link to redeem the offer** and **Terms and conditions** (on an offer)                     |

Each platform's page lists its options in full: [Mastodon](/platforms/mastodon#options), [Bluesky](/platforms/bluesky#options), [Threads](/platforms/threads#options), [X](/platforms/x#options), [LinkedIn](/platforms/linkedin#options), [Facebook Pages](/platforms/facebook#options), [Instagram](/platforms/instagram#options), [TikTok](/platforms/tiktok#options), [YouTube](/platforms/youtube#options), [Pinterest](/platforms/pinterest#options), [Google Business Profile](/platforms/google-business#options).

The post's text becomes the YouTube video's description, the TikTok post's caption, the Pinterest Pin's description, the LinkedIn post's commentary and the Google Business Profile post's description. Separate tags with commas. YouTube counts the commas, and two more for a tag with a space in it.

### Thumbnails and covers [#thumbnails]

A thumbnail or a cover is a file from the library that the post doesn't show. It follows these rules:

- It must be ready, and belong to the workspace.
- It can't be deleted while a post that goes out names it.
- A draft loses it when it's deleted, and a copy of the post leaves it out once it's gone.
- A channel set or a channel's default options can't name one.

### Options through the API [#options-api]

Through the API, a draft takes its options in `options`, one entry per channel, each tagged by its `platform`. Each of the post's targets answers with its own.

Updating a draft replaces its options, so any you leave out are removed. A channel you add only when you schedule the post has no options yet. On YouTube, TikTok or Pinterest, give it its options in the draft first.

### Stories and reels [#formats]

On Instagram and Facebook, **Format** sets what kind of post a channel receives. Peal never guesses it from the file. A vertical video stays a feed post (a reel, on Instagram) unless you select **Story**, or **Reel** on Facebook.

Each format takes its own media:

| Format    | Where     | What it takes                                                                                                                 | The post's text |
| --------- | --------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | --------------- |
| **Story** | Instagram | One JPEG image of 8 MiB at most, or one MP4 or QuickTime video of 3 to 60 seconds and 100 MiB at most                         | Not sent        |
| **Story** | Facebook  | One JPEG, PNG or GIF image of 10 MiB at most, or one vertical MP4 video (9:16, 540 pixels a side at least) of 3 to 60 seconds | Not sent        |
| **Reel**  | Facebook  | One vertical MP4 video (9:16, 540 pixels a side at least) of 3 to 90 seconds                                                  | Its description |

A story is sent without the post's text. The composer says so under the counters and counts nothing there, and a check through the API answers `textNotSent` for that channel. A story takes no other option either: no collaborators, none of a reel's settings, no title. A Facebook reel's title is the **Video title**.

A few more rules apply to both:

- A story or a reel has no parts after it.
- A story and the feed post of the same file and text don't count as duplicates of each other. Two stories of the same file do, whatever text the posts hold, because a story shows none.
- A story disappears after 24 hours. Peal keeps no link to it, and reads no statistics or comments from it.
- You can't boost a story or a reel from Peal. Each counts in the month's posts like any other.

A reel or a video story shows **Published** only once Meta says it's published. Peal reads Meta's status for up to 10 minutes after sending it, and a refusal Meta shows meanwhile shows as **Failed** (`processingFailed`).

If it's still not published after that, or Peal doesn't hear whether a reel or a story went out, the channel shows **To check** (`stillProcessing` when Meta was still processing it). Peal never sends it again. Look on the Page, then say what you found.

<Accordion>
<AccordionItem title="Frame rate, size and Meta's daily limit">

Meta asks a reel's and a video story's video to run at 24 to 60 frames a second, which Peal doesn't check. Meta refuses a video outside that range, or below 540p, while it processes it.

Meta also takes 30 reels a Page within any 24 hours through its API. Past that, the reel fails with Meta's code, and you can post it again later.

</AccordionItem>
<AccordionItem title="When the server restarts during a reel or a story">

If Peal's server restarts while a Facebook reel or story is going out, Peal carries on where it stopped. It noted the video or photo it sent before publishing it, so it reads Meta's status again for up to 10 minutes. It sends the reel or story again only when its note says it was never published.

A reel or story the server had begun before an upgrade to this release shows **To check**, and so does one it can't tell about. That may also mean it never went out. An Instagram story cut short this way shows **To check** too, so you can check the account, then confirm it or post it again. Peal never sends a story or a reel twice.

</AccordionItem>
</Accordion>

Through the API, a channel's capabilities list its platform's formats in `postFormats`, each with the media it takes and whether the text is sent.

## Media [#media]

A post can show images and videos from the workspace's media library. [Images and videos](/guides/media) explains how to upload and attach them, how to describe them, and what each platform accepts.

## From an agent, the command line or the API [#api]

An [MCP client](/developers/mcp/tools#tools), the [command line](/developers/cli/posts#posts) and the [HTTP API](/developers) do what the composer does, through the same checks. They can write a draft, schedule it, publish it now, move it, take a channel off it, take it back to draft and cancel it.

`POST /api/v1/posts` creates a post and sends it in one request, now or at a given instant. If the post breaks a rule of one of its channels' platforms, the request refuses the whole post and lists every violation at once. [Posting through the API](/developers/posting) shows how to check a post first, send several at once, import them from a CSV file and list them.

An application you connected schedules and publishes only if you let it. Publishing is a permission of its own, and you can leave it out so the application writes drafts for you to review. An API key does only what its permissions allow. [Posting from an AI agent](/developers/agents) explains both, with an example.
