A draft is a post with no time yet. You'll find drafts on their own list, every post on another, and you can schedule up to 100 at once from a CSV file.

## Drafts [#drafts]

To see them, click **Posts** in a workspace's sidebar, then **Drafts**. The list shows posts without a time, newest first, 20 at a time. Each one shows the start of its text, its channels and its media, and opens in the composer.

To drop a draft, click **Discard the draft** in the composer and confirm. The draft is deleted, and its images and videos stay in the media library. Owners, admins and editors can discard any draft. A contributor can discard the ones they wrote.

On the command line ([Drafts](/developers/cli/posts#drafts)):

```sh
peal drafts list                  # newest first; --limit and --cursor page through them
peal drafts discard <post-id>
```

## All posts [#all-posts]

To see every post of a workspace, whatever its state, click **Posts** in its sidebar. The sidebar then shows **All posts**, the list that opens. Posts come newest first, and **Show more** loads the next page. Each one opens on its page and shows:

- the start of its text
- its time in your time zone
- its status and, once it's finished, its outcome
- its channels and its tags

You can filter by status, by outcome (for finished posts), by channel and by tag. You can order by creation or by time, and when you order by time, the list holds only the posts that have one. The filters are kept in the page's address, so a link shows the same list.

## Import posts from a file [#import-screen]

On **All posts**, click **Import from a file** to schedule up to 100 posts at once from a CSV file, one post a row. Importing needs the right to schedule, so owners, admins and editors can do it.

The page lists the columns the file takes and offers a sample to start from. It also lists the workspace's channels with their ids, to copy into the file's `channels` column, and its channel sets' names. [Import posts from a CSV file](/developers/posting/bulk#import) describes each column and every answer.

<Steps>
<Step title="Choose a file">

Peal checks every row and makes nothing yet. Each row shows as valid, refused with the reason, or unreadable with the cells at fault.

</Step>
<Step title="Correct it until every row is valid">

It's all or nothing: **Import** is offered only once every row is valid. Until then, correct the file and choose it again.

</Step>
<Step title="Import">

Click **Import**. Peal asks you to confirm and says how many posts it'll schedule, then makes them in order and links each one.

</Step>
</Steps>

<Callout type="warning" title="A row dated now goes out at once">
  A row dated `now` is published as soon as its post is made, in public. The
  other rows wait for their time.
</Callout>

### If a row is refused during the import [#import-refused]

A row can still be refused during the import when something changed since the check, such as the month's room on the plan. The posts of the other rows are still made, before it and after it, and the page tells you which rows weren't.

<Callout type="warning" title="Refused rows go in a file of their own">
  Correct the refused rows and put them in a new file. If you import the same
  file again, the other rows' posts are made twice.
</Callout>

### On the command line [#import-cli]

`--dry-run` only checks the file. `--idempotency-key` makes the import safe to run again within 24 hours ([Posts](/developers/cli/posts#posts)):

```sh
peal posts import posts.csv --dry-run
peal posts import posts.csv --idempotency-key spring-campaign-2026-11
```
