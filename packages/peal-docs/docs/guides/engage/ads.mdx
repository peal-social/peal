Once an ad account on Meta or Google Ads is connected, you'll see what its campaigns and ads did next to what your posts did on their own. You can also boost a post you published through Peal as an ad on Facebook or Instagram.

<Callout type="note" title="Ads are new, and off by default">
  This feature is new, and its API may still grow. It's off unless the
  instance's operator turned it on, and an instance may offer reading without
  spending. Nothing spends until an owner allows an ad account to, with a
  monthly ceiling.
</Callout>

## What works today [#what-works]

With ads turned on, you can:

- Connect Meta ad accounts through a sign-in of their own, separate from the one that connects Facebook Pages and Instagram accounts as [channels](/guides/channels).
- Connect [Google Ads accounts](#google), read only: their campaigns and each campaign's daily cost, impressions and clicks.
- Read each ad account's campaigns, ad sets and ads, including those made outside Peal, and what each ad did each day.
- Compare organic and paid: a published post's own counts on each channel, beside what the ads that promote it did.
- [Boost a published post](/guides/engage/boosts) on a Facebook Page, or on an Instagram account connected through its Page, with a budget, an end date and an audience.
- Pause, resume and stop a boost.
- Follow every boost: the workspace's owners get notifications, your own systems get [webhook events](/developers/ads#webhooks), and every decision about money goes in an [audit trail](/guides/engage/boosts#audit).

The [API](/developers/ads#api), the SDKs, the [command line and MCP tools](/developers/ads#clients) do the same, except that the MCP tools don't offer Google Ads accounts. [What isn't built](#not-built) lists the rest.

## Who can do what [#access]

Ads have four permissions. A member holds those of their [role](/guides/posts#roles):

| Permission      | What it allows                                                                                                                        | Who holds it               |
| --------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------- |
| `ads:read`      | See the ad accounts, their campaigns and numbers, a post's paid results, and the boosts                                               | Owners, admins and editors |
| `ads:manage`    | Sign in to an ad network, connect and disconnect ad accounts, and pause or stop a boost                                               | Owners and admins          |
| `ads:spend`     | Boost a post, resume a paused boost, and pause or stop one                                                                            | Owners and admins          |
| `ads:authorize` | Decide whether an ad account may spend through Peal, set its monthly ceiling, and read the [audit trail](/guides/engage/boosts#audit) | Owners only                |

Two things take a person signed in to Peal in their own session. An API key, an application or staff acting as a member can't do them:

- Signing in to the ad network and choosing the ad accounts to connect (`ads:manage`). Only the person who signed in picks accounts from their sign-in.
- Allowing an ad account to spend, and setting or changing its ceiling (`ads:authorize`).

An [API key](/guides/workspace#api-keys) does what its ads permissions allow, and it never holds `ads:authorize`. An OAuth application also needs the `workspace:ads` scope, which opens nothing on its own. It goes with `workspace:write` for what `ads:manage` allows an application, and with `workspace:publish` for `ads:spend` ([What each permission allows](/developers/agents#scopes)). Staff acting as a member can only read.

## Connect ad accounts [#connect]

In a workspace, click **Ads** in the sidebar.

<Steps>
  <Step title="Sign in to Meta">
    An owner or an admin clicks **Connect an ad account** and signs in on Meta's
    page. Peal asks to read ads. On an instance that offers spending, it also
    asks for what making and running ads takes. You can decline that part, and
    the accounts are then read only, with no boosts.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Review the ad accounts">
    Back in Peal, you'll see the ad accounts the sign-in reaches, each with its
    currency, time zone, status and spend cap. Nothing is connected yet.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Pick the accounts">
    Tick the accounts the workspace uses, and connect them. You can connect up
    to 25 at once.
  </Step>
</Steps>

Only the person who started a sign-in can complete it, once, within 15 minutes. A sign-in that connected no account is removed after a day. Peal stores the sign-in's access token encrypted and never shows it.

Spending is off on every newly connected account. An account whose time zone Peal doesn't know can't be connected.

When the network refuses a sign-in's token, its accounts show that they need reconnecting, and the members who manage ad accounts are [told](/guides/engage/boosts#notices). Peal reads nothing from those accounts, and boosts nothing there, until the person who signed in does it again.

### Disconnect [#disconnect]

To disconnect one ad account, click **Disconnect** on it. To remove a whole sign-in, click **Remove the sign-in**. Here's what each one does:

- Disconnecting an ad account removes what Peal read of it.
- Removing a sign-in removes its token and every account it connects.
- The records of their boosts stay with the workspace. What they committed in a month still counts against the ceiling if the same account is connected again.
- Anything running at the network that Peal didn't make is left as it is.

<Callout type="warning" title="A running boost blocks the disconnect">
  Peal refuses both while one of their boosts runs, may run, is paused or is
  being made (`AdAccountBusy`), because a paused campaign can run again. Stop
  the boost first.
</Callout>

## What Peal reads [#reading]

Every hour, Peal reads each connected Meta ad account. (Google Ads accounts are read [every six hours, by campaign](#google).) It reads the campaigns, ad sets and ads, and each ad's numbers for each day of the account's calendar:

| Number      | What it is                                                                                              |
| ----------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Spend       | What the ad cost, in the account's [minor units](/guides/engage/boosts#money)                           |
| Impressions | How often it was shown                                                                                  |
| Reach       | How many people saw it that day. Summed over several days, someone seen on several days counts each day |
| Clicks      | Clicks on the ad                                                                                        |
| Results     | Reactions, comments and shares the network counts on the post. Missing, not zero, when it reported none |

The first read of an account covers the last 90 days. Later reads go over the last 7 days again, because networks correct recent days for a while.

A read stops once the account reaches 75% of the rate limit its network gives it, and carries on at a later hour. Peal never uses an ad account's whole allowance.

A day of numbers is kept 396 days (13 months), so you can compare a month with the same month a year before.

The numbers are the network's own, read with a delay of up to an hour or more. They aren't an invoice: what the network bills is in the ad account. Peal keeps counts and money, and it never reads who saw an ad.

### Organic and paid for a post [#post-performance]

A published post's page shows two sides, **Organic** and **Paid**:

- Organic is its counts on each channel, as [Analytics](/guides/engage/analytics) last read them.
- Paid is what the ads that promote it did, by ad account, with its boosts. An ad made outside Peal that promotes the same post counts too.

The two are never added together, because what a platform counts on a post already includes what its ads brought. Amounts in different currencies aren't added either, so you get one paid total per ad account.

The organic side is empty if you can't read analytics. It's also empty for a platform whose counts Peal [doesn't read](/guides/engage/analytics#schedule), such as Facebook Pages.

## Google Ads [#google]

Peal reads Google Ads accounts and never changes anything there. It creates, edits and pauses nothing at Google, and you can't boost a post on Google Ads or turn spending on for one of its accounts.

### Connect a Google Ads account [#google-connect]

On an instance whose operator [offers Google Ads](/self-hosting/ads#google), go to **Ads** and click **Connect an ad account on Google Ads**. Peal first tells you what it reads. Click **Continue to Google** to go on.

Google's consent screen then asks to see, edit, create and delete your Google Ads data, because Google has no reading-only access. Peal still only reads.

Google asks you to sign in with a passkey. Add one to your Google account before you start, because Google can take up to seven days to accept a new passkey.

Peal then lists every account your Google account reaches, each once: the accounts you use directly, and the client accounts of the manager accounts you use. When a manager account no longer holds one of them, Peal looks once for another way your sign-in reaches it. If there's none, the account shows as unreachable from that day, and what Peal already read stays.

### What Peal reads from Google Ads [#google-reading]

For each account, Peal reads:

- Its name.
- Its campaigns: name, status, type (such as `SEARCH` or `PERFORMANCE_MAX`), budget, start and end.
- Each campaign's cost, impressions and clicks for each day of the account's calendar.

Nothing about the people who saw an ad is read. Peal doesn't read ads, ad groups, keywords or conversions either, so a Google Ads account has no ads list. Reach and results are left out, because Google Ads doesn't report them this way.

Peal reads every six hours, so what it shows is never more than six hours behind. Each account's page shows the last read. The first read takes the last 90 days, and later ones the last 7, because Google corrects recent days.

Peal keeps cost the way Google writes it, in millionths of the currency, and rounds each figure it shows up to the cent once. A total can be a cent off Google's own. A removed campaign that did nothing over the days shown is left out of the list.

A Google Ads account's numbers stand on their own, on its page. They're never added to Meta's or to your organic numbers.

### Disconnect Google Ads [#google-disconnect]

When you disconnect the account, or remove its sign-in, Peal deletes what it read of it.

If you remove Peal's access in your Google account's security settings instead, Peal stops reading and deletes nothing. The sign-in shows as needing reconnecting, and what Peal read stays until you remove the sign-in.

## Your data [#data]

Here's what happens to ads data when something is deleted or exported:

| When                                               | What happens                                                                                                                                                                                                       |
| -------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| A workspace is deleted                             | Its sign-ins with their tokens, its ad accounts, what was read of them, its boosts, their journal and its audit trail are deleted. Deleting is refused while a boost runs, may run or is being made: stop it first |
| You close your account                             | Your id is removed from what stays with the workspaces you shared. The sign-ins you made stay with their ad accounts, without it, until the account is disconnected or the workspace deleted                       |
| You download your data                             | The [copy of your data](/guides/workspace/account#export) has an `ads` object for each workspace you're the only member of: its sign-ins without their tokens, its ad accounts, its boosts and its audit trail     |
| Someone asks Meta to delete what an instance holds | Peal removes the sign-ins they made and the ad accounts these connect                                                                                                                                              |
| Someone removes the app from their Meta account    | Their sign-ins are marked to renew                                                                                                                                                                                 |

What an ad needs goes to the ad network: the post's id, the budget, the dates, the audience, and the beneficiary and payer.

## What isn't built [#not-built]

Peal doesn't do any of this:

- Ad networks other than Meta's and Google Ads.
- On Google Ads, anything beyond reading campaigns and their daily numbers: no ads, conversions or boosts.
- Creating campaigns, ad sets or ads with creatives of their own. Peal boosts a post that's already published.
- Boosting a post that wasn't published through Peal, or one on an Instagram account connected with Instagram Login.
- Changing a running boost's budget or audience. Stop it, and make another.
- Interests, custom audiences, gender, or locations finer than countries.
- Special ad categories: housing, employment, financial products and services, social issues, elections or politics.
- Conversions, lead forms and automated rules.
