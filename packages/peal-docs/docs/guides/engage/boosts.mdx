A boost runs a post the workspace published through Peal as an ad on Facebook or Instagram. Before you start, [connect](/guides/engage/ads#connect) the ad account, and have an owner allow it to spend.

<Callout type="warning" title="A boost is billed to the ad account">
  The ad network bills the advertiser directly, on the ad account's payment
  method. Peal never handles the money, and takes no payment details for ads.
</Callout>

## Start a boost [#boosts]

A boost promotes one post the workspace published through Peal, on one ad account, as an ad that aims for engagement. Only a member who may spend can boost a post. On Peal Cloud, each ad active in a month counts against its billing account's monthly allowance of managed ads ([Usage meters](/guides/workspace/billing#meters)).

<Steps>
  <Step title="Open the post">
    Open the published post's page, and click **Boost**.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Choose where it runs">
    Choose the channel and the ad account.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Set the boost">
    Set the budget, the start and the end, the audience, the category, and the
    beneficiary and payer where needed. The table below gives the rules.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Check the quote">
    Click **Review**. Peal shows a quote: what the boost commits, the days it
    touches, the ceiling, what the account's other boosts already commit, and
    the room the account's own cap leaves. A quote checks everything and
    reserves nothing.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Confirm">
    Click **Spend up to … until …**, which shows your amount and end date. The
    boost is accepted as `requested`, and its commitment counts against the
    ceiling.
  </Step>
</Steps>

Each part of a boost has its rules:

| What                  | Rule                                                                                                                                                                                                               |
| --------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| The post              | Published through Peal on a Facebook Page, or on an Instagram account connected through its Facebook Page. Not an Instagram account connected with Instagram Login. Not a [story or a reel](/guides/posts#formats) |
| The budget            | `total` for the whole run, or `daily` for each day of it, above zero, in the ad account's currency                                                                                                                 |
| The end               | Required. At least a day after the start (more than a day with a daily budget), 90 days at most                                                                                                                    |
| The start             | Now, or up to 30 days ahead                                                                                                                                                                                        |
| The audience          | 1 to 25 countries, and an age range from 18 to 65, where 65 means "65 and over". Nothing finer                                                                                                                     |
| The category          | The advertiser declares the ad is of no special category. An ad about housing, employment, financial products and services, or social issues, elections or politics is refused                                     |
| Beneficiary and payer | Who benefits from the ad and who pays for it, 512 characters each. Required when any country of the audience is in the European Union                                                                              |

Peal then makes the boost in the background. It creates the campaign, its ad set and its ad paused, then turns them on, the campaign last. The post's page and the [notifications](#notices) tell you how that ended.

The ad network may still review the ad before it shows it, and it can refuse or stop the ad by its own rules.

### Pause, resume and stop [#control]

Each boost on the post's page has three actions:

| Action     | What it does                                                                                                                                                 |
| ---------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| **Pause**  | Tells the network to pause the boost's campaign. The boost is `paused` once the network confirms. Its budget stays counted.                                  |
| **Resume** | Runs a paused boost again until its end. It spends, so it's checked as a new boost is: spending still allowed, the account usable, the ceiling as it now is. |
| **Stop**   | Ends a boost for good. One that hasn't been made yet never will be. A stopped boost doesn't run again.                                                       |

Pausing and stopping are always available, both to those who may spend and to those who manage ad accounts. If you ask twice, they're done once. They take effect when the network applies them.

When the network doesn't answer, the boost stays where it was (`AdNetworkUnavailable`), and it's safe to ask again later. You can always pause or delete a boost at the network itself.

### States [#states]

A boost is always in one of these states:

| State         | What it means                                                                                                                                    |
| ------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| `requested`   | Accepted, and being made. Paused at the network, spending nothing                                                                                |
| `active`      | Running                                                                                                                                          |
| `paused`      | Paused by a member                                                                                                                               |
| `ended`       | Its end date passed                                                                                                                              |
| `stopped`     | Stopped by a member, for good                                                                                                                    |
| `failed`      | It couldn't be made, and nothing ran. `failureReason` says why                                                                                   |
| `unconfirmed` | Peal told the network to run it and couldn't read back whether it does. It may be spending. Its budget stays counted, and stopping it settles it |

<Callout type="warning" title="An unconfirmed boost may be spending">
  Peal never sends an unconfirmed boost again on its own. Someone has to check
  it, on the post's page or in the ad account, and stop it if it mustn't run.
</Callout>

### Why a boost failed [#failure-reasons]

A failed boost spent nothing. Its `failureReason` is one of these:

| `failureReason`       | What happened                                                                                  |
| --------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| `accountUnusable`     | The ad account can't run ads now.                                                              |
| `accountSpendCap`     | The account's own spend cap leaves too little room.                                            |
| `ceilingExceeded`     | The monthly ceiling was lowered meanwhile, and leaves too little room.                         |
| `spendingOff`         | Spending was turned off for the ad account meanwhile.                                          |
| `currencyMismatch`    | The budget's currency isn't the ad account's.                                                  |
| `budgetTooLow`        | The budget is under the network's minimum.                                                     |
| `postNotPromotable`   | The post was deleted, or can't be shown as an ad.                                              |
| `permissionMissing`   | The sign-in lacks a permission ads need.                                                       |
| `reconnectNeeded`     | The network refused the sign-in. The person who made it renews it by signing in again.         |
| `networkUnavailable`  | The network kept refusing for now. Peal tries a few times over about two hours, then gives up. |
| `refused`             | The network refused for a reason of its own, which `networkCode` carries.                      |
| `spendingUnavailable` | The instance no longer offers spending.                                                        |

## Money [#money]

Every amount is an integer in the minor units of the ad account's currency, as the network counts them. That's cents for `EUR` and `USD`, and whole yen for `JPY`. To work with amounts:

- Read the ad account's `minorUnitDigits`, 2 or 0, to show an amount: divide by 10 to that power, and add the `currency` (an ISO 4217 code). `5000` is 50.00 € on a `EUR` account, and 5,000 ¥ on a `JPY` one.
- Do the arithmetic on the integers, never on decimals.
- Give a boost's budget in its ad account's currency. A budget in another currency is refused.

### What a boost commits [#commitment]

Before a boost is accepted, Peal counts the most it can cost, its commitment:

| Budget  | What it commits                                                                                                                                                                                        |
| ------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| `total` | Its amount                                                                                                                                                                                             |
| `daily` | For each calendar week of the account's time zone (Sunday to Saturday) the run touches, its amount and 75% more for each day of the run in that week, and never more than 7 times its amount in a week |

Meta's page "About daily budgets" explains why. On any day, the network may spend up to 75% over a daily budget, so 175% of it. In a week ending on Saturday at midnight, it never spends more than 7 times the daily budget.

So a daily budget of 10.00 € over a run of two days commits 35.00 €. Over a whole week, Sunday to Saturday, it commits 70.00 €.

### The monthly ceiling [#ceiling]

An owner allows an ad account to spend through Peal, with a monthly ceiling in the account's currency. The ceiling is the most that the boosts made through Peal may commit on that account in one calendar month of its time zone. Here's how it holds:

- A boost that would pass it is refused before anything is reserved or sent (`SpendCeilingExceeded`). Peal checks the fullest month the run touches.
- You can always lower a ceiling, even under running boosts. They run on, and nothing new starts or resumes until there's room.
- Turning spending off is refused while a boost runs or may run. Stop it first.
- Peal never turns spending on, raises a ceiling or raises a budget on its own.

When a boost stops, its commitment is counted down to what it can have cost. That's nothing if it never ran, the days it ran for a daily budget, and its whole budget for a total one.

<Callout type="warning" title="The ceiling only limits Peal's boosts">
  It isn't a cap on the ad account itself. Ads made elsewhere, at the network or
  with another tool, aren't counted and aren't held back. See [the account's own
  spend cap](#spend-cap).
</Callout>

### The account's own spend cap [#spend-cap]

An ad account may have a spend cap of its own, set at the network, which covers everything the account spends. Peal reads it, with what the account spent against it. It refuses a boost the cap leaves too little room for (`AdAccountSpendCapExceeded`).

You raise the cap at the network, and Peal never does. To limit an ad account as a whole, set this cap, because the monthly ceiling only limits Peal's own boosts.

## Safety rules [#safety]

Everything that spends follows the same rules, whatever asks for it:

- Off by default, twice. Ads are off unless the instance's operator turns them on. Spending is off on each ad account until an owner turns it on, with an explicit monthly ceiling.
- Only an owner decides what may be spent, in person. Peal never turns spending on, raises a ceiling or raises a budget on its own.
- Everything is checked before anything is reserved or sent: the boost's own rules, the ceiling, the account's spend cap, the account's status and its sign-in.
- An end date is required. No boost runs without one, or for more than 90 days.
- A boost is created paused and turned on last, so nothing can spend before the whole boost exists as it was asked.
- Nothing is sent twice blindly. When a request's answer is lost, Peal looks it up at the network before sending anything again. If Peal still can't tell, the boost is `unconfirmed`, and a person decides.
- Pause and stop are always there, for those who spend and those who manage ad accounts.
- Every decision is recorded in the [audit trail](#audit), with who made it and the amounts.

## Notifications [#notices]

The workspace's owners hear about each boost, in the app and by email, when:

- It started.
- It ended.
- It used its whole budget.
- It couldn't be made.
- Peal couldn't confirm that it runs.

The members who manage ad accounts hear when an ad network sign-in must be renewed. These emails are always sent, because an owner answers for what is spent. [Notifications](/guides/workspace/notifications#events) lists them with the others.

## The audit trail [#audit]

Every decision about spending and every change to a boost is recorded, with who made it and the amounts:

- An ad account connected or disconnected
- Spending turned on or off
- A ceiling changed
- A boost requested, started, unconfirmed, failed, paused, resumed, stopped or ended

Each line names a member or an API key, and the application that acted for the member when there was one. What Peal's own jobs found, such as an end, names nobody.

Only owners can read the trail (`ads:authorize`). It shows the newest lines first, 100 lines a page. A line is kept 396 days (13 months). Once a member closes their account, their lines stay without their name.
