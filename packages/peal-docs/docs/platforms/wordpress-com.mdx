How to connect a site on WordPress.com, or a Jetpack site you reach there, and what an article there may hold. A site takes [articles](/guides/channels/websites), not posts, as a WordPress site of your own does.

## At a glance [#at-a-glance]

|             |                                                      |
| ----------- | ---------------------------------------------------- |
| Title       | 500 characters at most                               |
| Tags        | 20 at most                                           |
| Categories  | 20 at most                                           |
| On the site | Published at once, a draft there, or scheduled there |
| Unpublish   | Moves the post to the site's trash                   |

## Connect [#connect]

In **Channels**, under **WordPress.com**, choose **Connect WordPress.com**, sign in on WordPress.com, and pick the site. You must be able to publish posts and upload files there: an Author, an Editor or an Administrator. Peal asks WordPress.com for access to that one site only, keeps the token it gives encrypted, and never sees your password.

**Channels** offers WordPress.com once the Peal server is set up for it (see [On your own server](#self-hosting)). Reconnecting a channel signs in again, and must reach the same site.

## Articles [#articles]

A site on WordPress.com gets an article as a WordPress site of your own does: its images go to the site's media library once, its tags and categories are found by name or made, and a lost answer is looked for among your posts before anything else. See [Websites and articles](/guides/channels/websites#send).

WordPress.com may share a post published there to the social accounts the site connected on WordPress.com, as the site is set up: Peal does not change it.

## On your own server [#self-hosting]

A self-hosted server offers WordPress.com once its app is registered: see [WordPress.com](/self-hosting/platforms#wordpress-com).
