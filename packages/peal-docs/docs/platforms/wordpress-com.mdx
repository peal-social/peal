You can connect a site hosted on WordPress.com, or a Jetpack site you reach there. Like a WordPress site of your own, it takes [articles](/guides/channels/websites) rather than posts.

## At a glance [#at-a-glance]

Here's what an article on WordPress.com can hold, and what Peal can do with it:

|             |                                                      |
| ----------- | ---------------------------------------------------- |
| Title       | 500 characters at most                               |
| Tags        | 20 at most                                           |
| Categories  | 20 at most                                           |
| On the site | Published at once, a draft there, or scheduled there |
| Unpublish   | Moves the post to the site's trash                   |

## Connect a site [#connect]

In **Channels**, under **WordPress.com**, click **Connect WordPress.com**, sign in on WordPress.com, and pick the site. You need to be able to publish posts and upload files there, as an Author, an Editor or an Administrator.

Peal asks WordPress.com for access to that one site only. It keeps the token WordPress.com gives encrypted, and never sees your password.

**Channels** offers WordPress.com once the Peal server is set up for it (see [On your own server](#self-hosting)). Reconnecting a channel signs you in again, and has to reach the same site.

## Articles [#articles]

A site on WordPress.com gets an article the same way a WordPress site of your own does:

- Its images go to the site's media library, once.
- Its tags and categories are found by name, or created.
- When WordPress.com's answer is lost, Peal looks for the article among your posts before anything else.

See [Websites and articles](/guides/channels/websites#send).

<Callout
  type="warning"
  title="A site that shares its new posts shares Peal's articles too"
>
  A site can be set up to share its new posts to its own social accounts (on
  WordPress.com, under Jetpack Social). Peal doesn't change the site's settings,
  so the site shares the article Peal publishes as well. If those social
  accounts are also connected to Peal and you post the article there from Peal,
  they get it twice. Turn the sharing off for them, or leave them out in Peal.
</Callout>

## On your own server [#self-hosting]

A self-hosted server offers WordPress.com once its app is registered: see [WordPress.com](/self-hosting/platforms#wordpress-com).
