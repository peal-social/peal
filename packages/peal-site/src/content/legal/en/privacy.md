## About this policy

This policy explains how Maxime Bourmaud EI ("we") processes personal data when you use the hosted Peal service at app.peal.social and this website. It follows the transparency requirements of the EU General Data Protection Regulation (GDPR), Articles 13 and 14.

It does not cover self-hosted instances of Peal. Whoever runs a self-hosted instance is the controller of the data it processes and is responsible for its own privacy notice.

## Personal data we process

These categories follow the service as it is designed.

- **Account data**: your name, email address and sign-in details. You sign in with your email address and a password of at least 12 characters, and can add a second step with an authenticator app; the command line and the applications you connect sign in through your account.
- **Organisation data**: the organisations you belong to, your role in each, and the invitations you send or receive.
- **Connected accounts**: the social media accounts you connect, their identifiers on each platform, and the access tokens the platforms issue to Peal. For a website, its address, name and icon, and the user name and application password Peal publishes with, stored encrypted. For a LinkedIn company page, once we read LinkedIn comments, also the identifier on LinkedIn of the member who connected it, who may have no account with us when they connect through a connect link: LinkedIn limits the calls each member's sign-in may make a day, and Peal counts them under a keyed digest of that identifier, never the identifier itself, kept 7 days.
- **Content**: the posts, media and schedules you create, the articles you write for an organisation's websites (each one's title, body, excerpt, tags, categories and featured image, what the site made of it and its address there, and who wrote it), with the name each media file had on the device it was uploaded from or at the address it was imported from, the publication history of each post, and how each post was created and scheduled (in the app, with an API key or through an application you connected, and by which member); per organisation and month, how many posts went out on X, and how many held a link. When a member asks the writing assistant to change a text, write a post from a subject or make an image, what they wrote is sent to the AI provider for that one answer, and we keep none of it; an image it makes is kept as the organisation's media, marked as made by AI. When an organisation brings its own image provider, the description of each image it asks for goes to that provider instead, with the organisation's own key, under that provider's terms; when it brings its own text provider, the texts and subjects its members send the writing assistant go to that provider instead, in the same way. For each, we keep the provider's address and model (and, for a text provider, the kind of API it speaks) and the key, stored encrypted and never shown again, until the organisation removes the provider or is deleted; and how many images or requests the organisation asked of it each day, until the organisation is deleted. Such a provider is the organisation's choice and acts for it: it is not one of ours. When such an image is attached to a post, Peal turns on the label each platform offers for content made with AI, which the member may change; the organisation that publishes it decides how it is disclosed, and answers for it.
- **Performance data**: the metrics the platforms report for your posts, and for the accounts you connected: their followers day by day, and their views where the platform counts them.
- **Advertising data**, when an organisation connects ad accounts: the sign-in a member makes to the ad network (their identifier there, the permissions they gave, when the sign-in ends, and its access token, stored encrypted); the ad accounts connected, as the network describes them (identifier, name, currency, time zone, status, spend cap and amount spent), the monthly ceiling an owner set, who connected each and who allowed it to spend; their campaigns, ad sets and ads as read from the network, and each ad's daily numbers (counts and money, never who saw an ad); the boosts asked for (the post, the budget, the dates, the audience as countries and an age range, who benefits from the ad and who pays for it when the audience is in the European Union, and who asked); and the audit trail of every decision about spending, with who made it and the amounts.
- **Webhooks**: the addresses an organisation has Peal call when something happens in it, with the values of the headers it chose for them (such as a token its receiving server asks for, stored encrypted and never shown again), and the log of each delivery (the event's identifiers, states and times, never what a post says, and how the receiving server answered).
- **Usage counts**: per organisation and day, how many posts went out on each platform and how many requests it made to the writing assistant, and, as the daily count found them shortly after the day ended, how many channels and members it had and how much media it stored. Where Peal bills per connected account: per billing account and day, the most accounts connected at once across its organisations, which its invoice is computed from and which is sent to the payment provider as a count, kept as long as the other usage counts. Counts only, never what a post says or which member did what.
- **Short links and their click counts**: when a post's author asks for its links to be shortened, each short link's code, the address it leads to (the organisation's own content), the post, channel and part it is in, and how many times it was followed each day. Counts only: nothing about the people who follow a link, neither their IP address, their browser, the page they came from nor their country.
- **Billing data**: your plan, subscription status and invoices; each order placed before paying, with the consent given and the version of its words; the trial each account and organisation started; each withdrawal and what it refunded; each request to cancel a contract or to withdraw from it, with the date and time we received it and what became of it, and, when it came through the form that needs no account, what you wrote there (your name, the email address for the acknowledgement, the contract, the day wanted). Payments are handled by Stripe. Stripe collects your card or bank details: we never receive them. We receive what Stripe tells us of your customer record, subscription, payments and invoices, such as their status and amounts.
- **Billing accounts**: which person pays for each organisation (the member who created it, its oldest owner for one created before, or the owner it was handed to), and so which organisations share their plan; the offers to hand an organisation to another of its owners (who made each, to whom, and until when); and whether our staff required a person to add a payment method. To keep the free tier to one per person, our staff can see which accounts on a platform are connected in organisations paid for by different people without a subscription, compared by the platform's own account identifiers; that comparison is made when they look, and is not kept.
- **Technical data**: IP addresses, browser details and logs produced when you use the service, and, for each connection that creates, changes or deletes content, its time, IP address and protocol.
- **Communications**: the messages you send us and the emails we send you.
- **Comments on your posts, and people who are not users**: when an organisation's post is live on a platform where Peal reads comments (Mastodon and Bluesky, and Facebook Pages, Instagram, YouTube and LinkedIn company pages once we turn them on), the comments people leave under it in public: each one's text, when it was written, what it answers, and its author's public handle, display name and identifier on the platform, as the platform gives them: to anyone on Mastodon and Bluesky, and on Facebook, Instagram and YouTube to the connected account, through the permissions it granted (Facebook gives a name and no handle, and may give no author at all; YouTube gives the name its author displays; on LinkedIn we keep the author's LinkedIn identifier and no name). On YouTube, whose policies let us keep what its API gives for 30 days without reading it again, a post's comments lose their text and their author 29 days after the last read that found all of them, until a read finds them again, and a channel disconnected takes its comments with it at once; this content is from YouTube, under its terms. On LinkedIn, whose terms let us keep other members' profile data 24 hours, a post's comments lose their text and their author 24 hours after the last read that found all of them, until a read finds them again. We read nothing else of these people: no picture, no profile, no list of followers, no private message (direct messages are below). We keep whether the organisation answered a comment, and the replies its members send from Peal. We process these comments for the organisation, which decides what it answers, and use them for nothing else: we build no profile of their authors, show them no advertising, take no automated decision about them and train no model on what they wrote. A comment deleted on its platform loses its text and its author in Peal the next time we read the post. If you commented under a post and want to exercise your rights, write to the organisation that published it or to us (see Contact). For these comments the organisation is the controller, and we process them on its behalf (Article 28); it relies on its legitimate interest in reading and answering what is said under its own posts (Article 6(1)(f)). As we can reach the people who comment only through the platform, this policy is how they are informed (Article 14(5)(b)).
- **Reviews of an organisation's locations, and people who are not users**: when an organisation connects a Google Business Profile location, and once we turn the reading of reviews on, the reviews people leave about that location on Google, as Google gives them to the connected account through the permission it granted: each one's stars, text, when it was written and last changed, the name its author shows on Google (none when they chose to stay anonymous), and how many photos it holds. We keep neither the photos nor the author's picture or profile. We keep the location's reply as Google shows it, whether the organisation marked a review answered, and the replies its members send from Peal. Google's policies let us store what its API gives for 30 days at most: we keep a review 29 days after it was written or last changed, then delete it with the replies sent to it; a review Google no longer shows is deleted at our next complete read, and all of a location's reviews are deleted at once when its channel is disconnected or its organisation deleted. We process these reviews for the organisation, which decides what it answers, and use them for nothing else: we build no profile of their authors, show them no advertising, take no automated decision about them and train no model on what they wrote; this content is from Google, under its terms. If you reviewed a location and want to exercise your rights, write to the organisation or to us (see Contact). For these reviews the organisation is the controller, and we process them on its behalf (Article 28); it relies on its legitimate interest in reading and answering what is said in public about its own locations (Article 6(1)(f)). As we can reach the people who write reviews only through Google, this policy is how they are informed (Article 14(5)(b)).
- **Direct messages to an organisation's accounts, and people who are not users**: when an organisation connects a Mastodon or Bluesky account, once we turn the reading of direct messages on, and only once a member of the organisation turns it on for that account, the private conversations people hold with that account, as the platform gives them to the connected account through the permissions it granted: for each conversation, the other accounts in it (their identifier, handle and the name they show), and for each message its text, who sent it, when, and how many files it carries. We keep neither the files nor anyone's picture or profile. We keep whether a member read a conversation, and the messages its members send from Peal. We keep each message 30 days after it was sent, a message sent from Peal 30 days after sending ended or it was left unconfirmed, and a conversation until its last message is that old; a message or a conversation its platform no longer shows is deleted within a day where we can read the conversation, and the account's list of conversations, whole (one too long for that goes by the 30 days), and all of an account's conversations are deleted at once when a member turns its messages off, its channel is disconnected or its organisation deleted. We process these messages for the organisation, which decides what it answers, and use them for nothing else: we build no profile of their authors, show them no advertising, take no automated decision about them, train no model on what they wrote and give them to no writing assistant; our staff do not read them. Within the organisation, only its owners, admins and editors read them; an API key only when an owner of the organisation, in person, gave it that permission, and an application connected through OAuth, such as an AI assistant, only once an owner of the organisation allowed it, the application's provider then processing what it reads under its own terms. Peal never starts a conversation: it answers those someone else began. If you wrote to an organisation's account and want to exercise your rights, write to the organisation or to us (see Contact). For these messages the organisation is the controller, and we process them on its behalf (Article 28); it relies on its legitimate interest in reading and answering the messages sent to its own accounts (Article 6(1)(f)). As we can reach the people who write only through the platform, this policy is how they are informed (Article 14(5)(b)).
- **Review data**: when a member sends someone a review link to a post, what that person, who needs no account, gives on the link's page: the name they choose, the comments they write, what they decide (an approval, a request for changes) and when. We take no email address from a reviewer, and send them nothing. Members' comments and decisions are kept with their account. We process a reviewer's data for the organisation that sent the link. We act as that organisation's processor for this data (Article 28).

Some data about you can come from someone else: the email address a member enters to invite you to an organisation; the name a reviewer gives on a review link; the comments people write on your published posts, with their public handle and display name, which the platforms send us; the reviews people write about your locations on Google, with the name they show there, which Google sends us; the direct messages people send to your accounts on Mastodon and Bluesky, with their handle and the name they show, which the platforms send us; and the figures the platforms give for the posts and accounts you connected.

## Whether you must provide your data

Your name, email address and a password are needed to create an account, as the contract requires; without them, no account can be created. Everything else is optional and needed only for the feature that uses it: a connected account to publish there, billing details to order a paid plan. The identification data listed under Retention is kept because French law requires it.

## Data from connected platforms

When you connect a social media account, the platform shares data with Peal within the permissions you grant. Peal uses that data to publish the posts you schedule and to show how they perform. On Mastodon and Bluesky, and on Facebook Pages, Instagram, YouTube and LinkedIn company pages once we turn them on and you allow it, Peal also reads the public replies to the posts it published for you, to show them to your organisation and let its members answer. Peal makes no other use of that data, and never sells it.

When a member signs in to an ad network to connect ad accounts, the network shares, within the permissions that member grants, the ad accounts they can reach, then the campaigns, ads and daily numbers of the accounts the organisation connects. Peal uses them to show what the ads did, and to run the boosts members ask for. The member who signed in can ask the network to delete what Peal holds about them: Peal then removes their sign-ins and the ad accounts these connect.

You can disconnect an account in Peal, from the organisation's Channels page: Peal then deletes the tokens it holds for it. You can also revoke Peal's access from the platform's own settings.

The identifier of the member who connected a LinkedIn page goes with the page: when it is disconnected or its organisation is deleted, when another member reconnects it, and within a day once we stop reading LinkedIn comments. Closing the Peal account of whoever connected it leaves it with the organisation, as the page stays there; LinkedIn sends no request to delete a member's data, so such a request reaches us at privacy@peal.social.

## International transfers

Peal's server, its database and its media are in the European Union. Some providers listed under Recipients and sub-processors are established in the United States or may reach the data from there: Cloudflare, for the network and bot protection; Resend, for email; Stripe, for payments; and Anthropic, for the writing assistant when it is on. Each of those transfers rests on the European Commission's adequacy decision for the EU-US Data Privacy Framework, for providers certified under it, and on the standard contractual clauses the Commission adopted (Article 46(2)(c)). You can ask privacy@peal.social for a copy of the safeguards.

## Your rights

Under the GDPR, you have the right to:

- access the personal data held about you (Article 15);
- have it corrected (Article 16);
- have it erased (Article 17): [Data deletion](/data-deletion/) explains how;
- restrict its processing (Article 18);
- receive it in a structured, machine-readable format (Article 20);
- object to processing based on legitimate interests (Article 21);
- withdraw your consent at any time where processing relies on it, without affecting the processing that came before (Article 7(3)).

Closing your account erases your data at once, except the identification data above, which French law requires us to keep (Article 17(3)(b)); your copy of your data lists it, and you can still ask for it by writing to privacy@peal.social. You can download a copy of your data at any time from your account settings, or with the command line; we email you each time a copy is downloaded, so a copy someone else took does not go unnoticed. To exercise these rights, write to privacy@peal.social. The GDPR requires an answer within one month, which can be extended in some cases (Article 12(3)). We act on a request sent from the email address of the account, or once its holder confirms it from that address. Where we still have reasonable doubts about who is asking, we may ask for what is needed to confirm it (Article 12(6)), and nothing more.

You also have the right to lodge a complaint with a supervisory authority, in particular in the EU country where you live, where you work or where you think the infringement took place (Article 77). In France, the authority is the Commission nationale de l'informatique et des libertés (CNIL), 3 place de Fontenoy, TSA 80715, 75334 Paris Cedex 07, www.cnil.fr.

No decision with legal or similarly significant effects on you is made solely by automated means (Article 22). A plan's limits are applied automatically, as the contract sets them; suspending an account is always a decision of a member of Peal's staff, recorded with its reason.

## Cookies and similar technologies

The app at app.peal.social uses only what it needs to work, so it asks no consent:

- the sign-in cookies, whose names start with `peal.`: your session, for 30 days and renewed each day you use Peal; a short-lived copy of it, for 5 minutes; the second sign-in step in progress, and the browser you chose to trust for it, for 30 days; and, for Peal's staff, the session they act in for an account, for an hour;
- `peal_connect_link`, while you connect an account through a connect link, for 30 minutes;
- `NEXT_LOCALE`, the language of the pages, until you close the browser;
- in the browser's storage: the time format you chose (`peal.hour-format`), and, until the tab is closed, the sign-in to a platform you started, so that Peal can finish it when you come back;
- Cloudflare Turnstile, on sign-up, password reset and review links, to tell people from bots.

This website and the documentation measure visits with Plausible, which sets no cookie and stores nothing in your browser. The documentation remembers your theme and language in your browser's storage.

## Security

Access tokens for connected accounts are stored encrypted with AES-256-GCM. Traffic is encrypted in transit. The server, in Paris, opens no port to the internet: it is reached only through an encrypted tunnel, and its administration and monitoring sit behind a sign-in reserved to Peal's staff. Passwords are stored hashed; a second sign-in step is available to everyone, and an organisation can require it of its members. Sign-ins, the API and public forms are rate-limited. Backups are encrypted before they leave the server, and kept in the EU. The server applies security updates automatically. Every action Peal's staff take on an account is recorded with its reason, and the identification data French law has us keep is readable only by staff who hold that role.

## Children

Peal is not meant for children. You must be at least 18, or of age where you live, to create an account. We do not knowingly process the data of anyone younger; if you learn that a child has given us their data, write to privacy@peal.social and we will delete it.
