## Summary

This summary is here to help you read the agreement. It is not part of it, and the sections below are what binds us.

- When your organisation uses Peal, some of what passes through it is personal data about other people: the comments under your posts, the messages sent to your accounts, the reviews of your locations, the names in your posts. You decide what happens to that data, and we process it for you.
- We do only what the service needs and what you ask through it. We don't sell that data, profile the people in it, show them advertising or train a model on it.
- The data is stored in the European Union. The providers we rely on are listed on the [sub-processors](/subprocessors/) page, and you hear about a new one 30 days ahead.
- If personal data is breached, we tell you without undue delay, with what you need to meet your own duties.
- When the contract ends, your data is deleted. You can take a copy before that.
- You don't have to sign anything: this agreement applies as soon as you accept the [terms of service](/terms/). Write to privacy@peal.social if you need a countersigned copy.

## About this agreement

This data processing agreement is part of the [terms of service](/terms/) between Peal SAS ("we"), 15 rue Paul Verlaine, 85710 La Garnache, France, and the customer that accepted them ("you"). It applies whenever we process personal data on your behalf in the hosted Peal service at app.peal.social, and it is the contract that Article 28(3) of the EU General Data Protection Regulation (GDPR) requires.

You accept it with the terms, and it needs no signature. If your own rules call for a signed copy, write to privacy@peal.social and we send one countersigned.

It does not cover self-hosted instances of Peal, where we process nothing. It does not cover the data we answer for ourselves either, such as your account, your invoices and our security logs: the [privacy policy](/privacy/) covers that.

Where this agreement and the terms disagree about personal data, this agreement applies.

## Definitions

"Controller", "processor", "personal data", "processing", "data subject", "personal data breach" and "supervisory authority" mean what they mean in the GDPR.

"Your personal data" is the personal data we process on your behalf under the terms. Annex I describes it.

"Sub-processor" is a provider we engage to process your personal data.

"Data protection law" is the GDPR, the French data protection act (loi n° 78-17 du 6 janvier 1978) and any other data protection law that applies to the processing.

<aside>

**What it means**

The words mean what the GDPR says they mean. "Your personal data" is the data about other people that you handle through Peal.

</aside>

## Roles

For your personal data, you are the controller and we are your processor. Where you yourself act for someone else, for example an agency that manages its clients' accounts, you are that client's processor and we are your sub-processor; you then pass on your client's instructions and answer to them for giving them.

You are responsible for having a legal basis for the processing, for informing the people concerned, and for the instructions you give us. You confirm that you may hand us the personal data you bring into Peal.

For the data listed in the [privacy policy](/privacy/) as ours, we are a controller in our own right, and this agreement does not apply.

<aside>

**What it means**

You decide what is done with the data about your audience and your contacts, and we carry it out. If you work for clients, the same holds one step further up. For your own account and billing, we answer for the data ourselves.

</aside>

## Your instructions

We process your personal data only on your documented instructions. These are the terms, this agreement, the settings you choose in the service, and what your members, API keys and connected applications ask of it. You can give further instructions in writing at privacy@peal.social; we follow them where the service allows it, and tell you when it doesn't.

If the law of the European Union or of a member state requires us to process your personal data in another way, we tell you before we do, unless that law forbids us to.

We tell you at once if we think an instruction breaks data protection law. We are not required to check your instructions against the law before following them.

We do not sell your personal data. We do not use it to profile the people it concerns, to show them advertising, to take an automated decision about them or to train a model.

<aside>

**What it means**

We do what the service needs and what you ask through it, and nothing else. If one of your instructions looks unlawful to us, we say so.

</aside>

## Confidentiality

The people at Peal who may reach your personal data are bound to keep it confidential, by contract or by law, and reach it only as far as their work needs. Every action our staff take on an account is recorded with its reason. Our staff do not read the direct messages sent to your accounts.

<aside>

**What it means**

Access to your data is limited to the people who need it, they are bound to secrecy, and what they do on an account leaves a record.

</aside>

Annex II lists them. We may change them as the service evolves, without lowering the level of protection.

You are responsible for the security of what you control: your members' passwords and second sign-in step, the roles you give them, your API keys and the applications you connect.

<aside>

**What it means**

We secure the service as Annex II describes. You secure your side: who is a member, with which role, and which keys and applications can act for you.

</aside>

## Requests from the people concerned

The service gives you what you need to answer most requests yourself: you can read, correct, export and delete your content, and disconnect an account, which deletes the tokens Peal holds for it.

If someone writes to us to exercise a right over your personal data, we pass the request to you without undue delay and do not answer it ourselves, unless you ask us to or the law requires it. Where the service does not let you answer a request, we help you with it, as far as the nature of the processing allows.

<aside>

**What it means**

Most requests you can handle from the app. If a person writes to us about data that is yours, we send them your way and help you where the app can't.

</aside>

## Help with your own obligations

Taking into account the processing and the information we have, we help you carry out a data protection impact assessment, consult a supervisory authority beforehand and meet your security duties, where the law asks that of you.

This help is included in the service. If a request calls for work well beyond it, we may charge our reasonable costs, agreed with you beforehand, unless the work is needed because of a fault of ours.

<aside>

**What it means**

If you need to assess the risks of using Peal, or to answer an authority, we give you the information we have. Ordinary help is free.

</aside>

## Personal data breaches

We notify you without undue delay after we become aware of a personal data breach that affects your personal data. We write to the owners of your organisation at their account's email address.

The notice describes what happened, the data and the people concerned and roughly how many, the likely consequences, what we did and plan to do about it, and whom to contact. If we don't have all of it yet, we send what we have and the rest as we learn it.

We take the measures needed to contain the breach and limit its effects, and we help you notify the supervisory authority and the people concerned where you must. Notifying you is not an admission of fault.

<aside>

**What it means**

If your data is breached, you hear from us fast, with what you need to tell the authority and the people affected.

</aside>

## Deletion and return

Until the contract ends, you can export your personal data from the service, in the app, with the API or with the command line.

When the contract ends, we delete your personal data, as the [data deletion](/data-deletion/) page describes and within the periods the [privacy policy](/privacy/#retention) sets. Deleted data leaves our encrypted backups once four newer weekly backups were made, within 5 weeks while backups run as scheduled.

We keep only what the law of the European Union or of a member state requires us to keep, for as long as it requires, and we process it for nothing else.

<aside>

**What it means**

Take a copy whenever you want. When you leave, your data is deleted, and it is gone from the backups about five weeks later.

</aside>

This agreement lasts as long as we process your personal data, and ends when that data is deleted.

Each party's liability under this agreement follows the "Warranties and liability" section of the terms. Nothing in this agreement limits what a party owes a data subject or a supervisory authority under data protection law.

French law governs this agreement, and disputes go to the courts the terms name. We change this agreement the way we change the terms: by email to account holders at least 30 days ahead, sooner when a law or an authority requires the change.

## Annex II: security measures

- Access tokens for connected accounts are stored encrypted with AES-256-GCM. The keys and passwords you give Peal to publish with are stored encrypted too.
- Traffic is encrypted in transit.
- The server, in Paris, opens no port to the internet. It is reached only through an encrypted tunnel, and its administration and monitoring sit behind a sign-in reserved to Peal's staff.
- Passwords are stored hashed. A second sign-in step is available to every member, and an organisation can require it.
- Sign-ins, the API and public forms are rate-limited.
- Backups are encrypted before they leave the server, and kept in the European Union.
- The server applies security updates automatically.
- Every action Peal's staff take on an account is recorded with its reason.
- Within an organisation, roles decide who reads and changes what. Direct messages are read only by owners, admins and editors, and by an API key or application only once an owner allowed it.
